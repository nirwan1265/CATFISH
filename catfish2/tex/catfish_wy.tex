\abstract{
\textbf{Motivation:} Pathway analysis of genome-wide association study (GWAS)
summary statistics must contend with two distinct sources of dependence. Genes
within a pathway are correlated through linkage disequilibrium (LD), and
pathways are correlated with one another through shared gene membership. Most
pipelines address only the first, calibrating each pathway against its own
LD-aware null and then applying a Bonferroni or Benjamini--Hochberg correction
that assumes the resulting pathway $p$-values are independent. They are not,
and the correction is consequently miscalibrated in a direction that depends on
how strongly the pathway family overlaps.\\
\textbf{Results:} We extend CATFISH, a correlation-aware omnibus pathway
framework combining ACAT, Fisher, adaptive soft TFisher, minP and Stouffer
statistics, with a Westfall--Young-style family-wise calibration. A single
genome-wide correlated null draw, generated from a multivariate normal model of
gene--gene correlation, is propagated through every pathway in the family; read
down its columns it gives each pathway a calibrated marginal $p$-value, and read
across its rows the minimum over pathways gives a family-wise threshold that
accounts for between-pathway dependence without modelling it. Numerical
precision is treated as a correctness requirement: in a GWAS of $n\approx1.3$
million individuals the omnibus statistic underflows double precision for the
majority of pathways, and the resulting ties silently destroy the ranking.
Across simulated null families spanning three LD regimes and three pathway
sizes, independence-based calibration was anti-conservative while per-pathway
and Westfall--Young calibration held type~I and family-wise error near nominal,
and both remained stable when up to four of the five component tests were
deliberately miscalibrated. Under five stylised signal architectures, the
component predicted to be sensitive to each was the best-performing component in
$\nArchCellsIn$ of $\nArchCellsTot$ architecture-by-size combinations. Applied to a maize
grain-nitrogen GWAS, the Westfall--Young ordering recovered L-homoserine
biosynthesis as the leading pathway and placed a known aspartate kinase isozyme
first among 34,873 genes. Applied to GLGC LDL- and HDL-cholesterol GWAS, it
exposed a regime in which the self-contained null is false almost everywhere:
11.2\% of genes reach genome-wide significance for LDL-C and essentially every
pathway is significant, so significance no longer discriminates. The ordering
still does: plasma lipoprotein assembly, remodeling and clearance ranked first
of $\nLipNpw$ Reactome pathways for both LDL-C and HDL-C, and the combined
gene score ranked \textit{LDLR} and \textit{ABCA1} first and recovered
established lipid genes better than ranking by GWAS association alone. In a
sorghum bioenergy panel of 330 lines, where gene--gene correlation is far
denser than in the human data, no pathway was significant for dry biomass, and
a phenotype-permutation null showed that centring the gene $z$-scores of each
GWAS calibrates every pathway, while the family-wise error stays above nominal
because the variant-level tests are heavy-tailed. We argue
that the saturation is a property of self-contained testing at very large $n$,
not a failure of multiple-testing correction.\\
\textbf{Availability:} CATFISH is an R package available at
\url{https://github.com/nirwan1265/CATFISH} under the MIT licence. All
simulation, analysis and figure code for this paper is in the
\texttt{catfish2/} directory of that repository.\\
\textbf{Contact:} \href{mailto:ntanduk@ncsu.edu}{ntanduk@ncsu.edu}\\
\textbf{Supplementary information:} Supplementary data are available at
\textit{Bioinformatics} online.}

\boxedtext{Key Messages}{
\begin{itemize}
\item Pathway $p$-values are correlated because pathways share genes;
Bonferroni and Benjamini--Hochberg corrections applied to them are therefore
miscalibrated, and the direction of the error depends on the overlap structure
of the particular pathway database used.
\item A Westfall--Young max-statistic null, built by propagating one
genome-wide correlated draw through the whole pathway family, controls the
family-wise error rate in the strong sense without assuming the pathways are
independent; its validity rests instead on the multivariate normal correlation
model used to generate the draw.
\item Implementing the omnibus in log space is a correctness requirement, not an
optimisation: at GWAS sample sizes above roughly one million the analytic
omnibus underflows double precision and the pathway ranking collapses into ties.
\item Self-contained pathway tests saturate at very large $n$. When 11\% of all
genes are genome-wide significant, a random 50-gene set contains several of
them, almost every pathway rejects, and exact FWER control does not restore
discrimination. Saturation is a property of the hypothesis being tested, not of
the correction applied to it.
\end{itemize}}

\section{1 Introduction}

Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have transformed complex trait research
by identifying thousands of loci linked to disease, development, and
quantitative phenotypes. Yet most variants explain only a small fraction of
phenotypic variance and often fall in noncoding regions, limiting single-marker
mechanistic interpretation \citep{Manolio2009,Boyle2017}. Complex traits are
often highly polygenic, with relevant signals distributed across many
modest-effect loci rather than concentrated in a few genome-wide significant
variants \citep{Akula2025,Lappalainen2024}. Gene-set analysis, including
pathway-based approaches, is therefore a key post-GWAS strategy for translating
summary statistics into biological hypotheses. By aggregating evidence across
predefined groups of functionally related genes, pathway-based methods enhance
interpretability, reduce the multiple-testing burden, and reveal coordinated
pathway disruptions that single-locus analyses may miss
\citep{Mooney2015,deLeeuw2015}. This is especially valuable for polygenic
traits, where many genes may influence the same phenotype through shared
biological processes. In such settings, a candidate gene becomes more compelling
when locus- or gene-level association evidence is reinforced by pathway-level
enrichment from a biologically relevant process. Pathway support therefore
provides functional context for prioritization, although it is best interpreted
as complementary rather than definitive evidence because pathway annotations
remain incomplete and often overlapping.

A plethora of pathway analysis tools has been developed for GWAS. Classical
over-representation methods test whether predefined pathways are enriched among
significant genes, as implemented in DAVID, g:Profiler, and WebGestalt
\citep{Sherman2022,Kolberg2023,Liao2019}. Ranking-based methods such as Gene Set
Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) assess whether pathway genes are non-randomly
concentrated at the top or bottom of a ranked gene list
\citep{Subramanian2005}. GWAS-focused extensions, including MAGENTA, INRICH, and
MAGMA, add features such as SNP-to-gene mapping, pathway-level modeling, and
adjustment for linkage disequilibrium (LD) or gene size
\citep{Segre2010,Lee2012,deLeeuw2015}. Accounting for LD is essential in GWAS
pathway analysis because nearby variants are often correlated and therefore do
not provide independent evidence of association. If LD is ignored, clusters of
linked SNPs can be effectively counted multiple times, leading to inflated
gene-level aggregation and enrichment biases toward larger genes or regions with
dense marker coverage. Several widely used post-GWAS methods therefore
explicitly account for LD through model-based or reference-based dependence
estimates rather than assuming independence. VEGAS uses multivariate normal
simulation, Pascal uses LD from a reference population to compute gene and
pathway scores, and MAGMA models gene-level dependence through an approximated
correlation structure
\citep{Liu2010,deLeeuw2015,Lamparter2016,Watanabe2017}.

These methods address dependence \emph{within} a pathway. A second source of
dependence operates \emph{between} pathways and is far less often handled.
Curated pathway databases are built from overlapping annotations: a gene that
participates in several metabolic steps is a member of several pathways, and
hierarchical resources such as Reactome place parent and child pathways in the
same family. The pathway-level $p$-values returned by any method are therefore
correlated with one another, sometimes strongly. Nonetheless, the final
multiple-testing step in essentially every published pipeline is a Bonferroni or
Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment of those $p$-values, both of which are derived
under independence or under positive-regression dependence conditions that
overlapping gene sets do not generally satisfy. The resulting family-wise error
rate is not the nominal one, and the size of the discrepancy is a property of
the particular database rather than of the method, which makes it invisible to
the kind of simulation study that generates independent pathways.

The classical solution to exactly this problem is the Westfall--Young
max-statistic procedure \citep{Westfall1993}. Rather than correcting each
$p$-value in isolation, one generates a null realization of the \emph{entire}
family of tests, records the single most extreme statistic observed in that
realization, and repeats. The distribution of that maximum is the reference
distribution for the observed minimum, and the resulting threshold controls the
family-wise error rate under the complete null whatever the dependence structure
among the tests, because the dependence is inherited from the way the null
realizations are generated rather than assumed. In a GWAS pathway setting this means drawing
correlated gene-level null statistics genome-wide, propagating that single draw
through every pathway in the family, and recording the minimum pathway
$p$-value. Nothing about the between-pathway correlation needs to be estimated,
modelled, or even named.

Here we extend CATFISH (Combining \textbf{C}auchy combination test (ACAT),
\textbf{A}daptive \textbf{T}Fisher (soft) test, \textbf{F}isher's test,
m\textbf{I}n-P, and \textbf{S}touffer's method for \textbf{H}olistic pathway
analysis), a correlation-aware pathway analysis framework that integrates
multiple complementary gene-set statistics in a unified omnibus strategy, with
Westfall--Young family-wise calibration. Different pathway tests are sensitive
to different genetic architectures: some are best at detecting broad,
distributed enrichment, whereas others are more sensitive to sparse strong
signals or heterogeneous evidence. Instead of relying on a single summary
statistic, CATFISH evaluates each pathway with several complementary tests and
combines them in an omnibus framework. This improves robustness when the true
signal configuration is unknown while preserving the interpretability of the
component tests. Beyond pathway-level inference, CATFISH prioritizes candidate
genes by integrating locus-level GWAS signals, gene-level MAGMA results, and
pathway-level support.

This paper makes three contributions beyond the original CATFISH framework.
First, we add the Westfall--Young layer described above and characterize how it
behaves: the same correlated null, read down its columns, supplies each
pathway's marginal $p$-value, and read across its rows, the family-wise one. Second, we show that the entire statistic must be computed in log
space. This sounds like an implementation detail and is not: at the sample sizes
now routine in human GWAS consortia, the analytic omnibus $p$-value for a
typical pathway falls below the smallest representable double-precision number,
every such pathway is assigned the same floor value, and the ranking that the
whole analysis exists to produce is destroyed without any error being raised.
Third, we report a result we believe is general. In GLGC LDL- and
HDL-cholesterol GWAS of roughly 1.3 million individuals, the self-contained null
hypothesis is false for almost every gene set that could be constructed and
nearly every pathway is significant, so family-wise significance no longer
discriminates between pathways. We interpret this as saturation of the
self-contained hypothesis rather than as a defect of the correction. The
ordering of pathways, read against their size, still recovers lipoprotein
metabolism in both traits, and the gene-level prioritization recovers the
established genes of each trait better than GWAS association alone. We discuss
what saturation implies for the choice between self-contained and competitive
testing as GWAS sample sizes continue to grow.

CATFISH is implemented as an R package and is freely available at
\url{https://github.com/nirwan1265/CATFISH} under the MIT licence.

\subsection*{1.1 Pathway signal archetypes}

A pathway can be associated with a trait in very different ways. Its evidence
may come from one extreme gene, from a few strong genes, from many genes of
moderate effect, or from a faint shift shared by nearly all of its members, and
the component tests of CATFISH are sensitive to different parts of that range.
We formalise the range as five pathway signal archetypes: Strong Sparse Signal
(SSS), Single-Gene Proxy (SGP), Moderate Dense Signal (MDS), Weak Diffuse
Enrichment (WDE) and Mixed Signal Architecture (MSA). They are statistical
templates for interpreting test behaviour, not empirically learned classes of
real pathways, and they are the reason CATFISH combines complementary components
instead of choosing one in advance. Section~3.1 defines the archetypes, relates
each to a biological setting in which it is expected, and tests the predicted
correspondence between archetype and component under the family-wise decision
rule used throughout this paper. Supplementary Note~S1 gives the expanded
descriptions and the simulation design.

\section{2 Methods}

\subsection*{2.1 Gene-level inputs and component tests}

For a pathway $S$ containing $m=|S|$ genes, let $p_g$ denote the gene-level
association $p$-value for gene $g\in S$ and $Z_g$ the corresponding gene-level $Z$-statistic. We write $\mathbf{p}_S=(p_1,\dots,p_m)$ and $\mathbf{Z}_S=(Z_1,\dots,Z_m)$. Gene-level statistics are obtained with MAGMA \citep{deLeeuw2015}, which aggregates SNP-level GWAS evidence across variants assigned to a gene while accounting for LD, gene size, and local marker density, and which additionally returns the gene--gene correlation matrix induced by LD. These quantities are the common inputs for every CATFISH component test and for both calibration layers.

Each pathway is assessed using five complementary component tests (Table~\ref{tab:components}): ACAT \citep{Liu2019ACATV,Liu2020ACAT}, Fisher \citep{Fisher1932}, adaptive soft-thresholding TFisher \citep{Zhang2020TFisher}, minP, and Stouffer \citep{Stouffer1949}. Together, they cover the signal archetypes of Section~3.1 and apply directly to gene-level $p$-values or $Z$-scores without external weights or pathway-specific tuning. We combine the component $p$-values into a single omnibus statistic via an ACAT-type cross-method operator $\mathcal{O}(\cdot)$. This omnibus is a pathway-level summary of evidence across components, not an additional gene-level test.

Four components (ACAT, Fisher, minP, Stouffer) are fully determined by gene-level $p$-values and require no tuning. Soft TFisher is the exception: it adapts over a grid of truncation thresholds $\tau$, which determines which genes contribute. This is the only tunable choice in CATFISH, and we justify it below.

Following \citet{Zhang2020TFisher}, the grid spans small, intermediate and large thresholds rather than concentrating at one end: we use \(\tau\in\{10^{-2},\,5\times10^{-2},\,5\times10^{-1}\}\) and take the minimum calibrated TFisher \(p\)-value over the grid. The original authors' grid also includes \(\tau=1\), which we omit because \(\tau=1\) is Fisher's method and Fisher is already carried as a separate component. Spanning the range matters because the number of genes surviving truncation is \(\mathrm{Binomial}(m,\tau)\): a grid confined to small \(\tau\) leaves \(m\tau\ll1\) at realistic set sizes, the statistic is then zero on a large fraction of pathways, and the component is degenerate precisely where it is most often applied.

A threshold is informative mainly when \(m\tau\) is not far below one, so which \(\tau\) values are active depends on set size: \(\tau=5\times10^{-1}\) is informative from the smallest sets upward, whereas \(\tau=10^{-2}\) requires \(m\approx100\). Adapting across \(\tau\) rather than fixing a single value is what allows one component to serve both sparse and diffuse architectures, and users can assess the grid for their own database using \(m\tau\).

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Undefined components.} When no gene in a pathway falls below any threshold in the grid, the soft TFisher statistic is zero at every \(\tau\) and its one-sided \(p\)-value is exactly one. We treat this as \emph{undefined} rather than as evidence against enrichment, and the omnibus for that pathway is formed from the components that are defined. The distinction is not cosmetic. The ACAT operator maps each component \(p\)-value through \(\tan\{(1/2-p)\pi\}\), so a value at the upper boundary contributes a large negative term; a component that could not be computed would otherwise vote against the pathway and bias the omnibus toward the null. The rule is applied per pathway rather than by set size, because a large pathway in which no gene is individually strong is equally uncomputable. It does condition the component on having at least one gene below a threshold, so where TFisher does contribute its \(p\)-values are stochastically small; because the resampling null is generated under the identical rule, that conditioning appears in the null distribution and is absorbed by the calibration. An analytic omnibus \(p\)-value under this rule would be anti-conservative, whereas the resampled one is not.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Precision limit.} The soft TFisher implementation returns the null distribution function of the soft-thresholding statistic, so the one-sided \(p\)-value is formed as one minus that value. In double precision the difference is exactly zero once the distribution function exceeds \(1-10^{-16}\), and a strongly enriched pathway would then report \(p=0\). We cap the soft TFisher \(p\)-value at \(10^{-16}\), the smallest value the computation can resolve, rather than at the general lower floor applied to components whose tails are computed in closed form. A cap below the precision limit asserts resolution the statistic does not have; Section~4.6 shows what that does to the family-wise threshold.

\subsection*{2.2 Two sources of dependence}

CATFISH statistics are correlated at two levels. The two arise from different
causes, and both have to be addressed before any pathway $p$-value means what
it claims to.

\emph{Within a pathway}, component tests are dependent for two reasons: each is a deterministic function of the same $\mathbf{p}_S$, inducing cross-method dependence even if genes were independent; and gene-level evidence is correlated via LD, overlapping SNP-to-gene assignments, and shared local genomic architecture. Thus, any analytic reference distribution assuming independence is wrong for every component and typically anti-conservative, because correlated genes contain less information than the same number of independent genes.

\emph{Between pathways}, pathway-level $p$-values are correlated because databases assign genes to multiple sets. A gene shared across pathways propagates its (possibly extreme) null draw to all of them, shifting their evidence in the same direction. The strength and pattern of this dependence depend on database structure: hierarchies with nested sets create widespread but weak dependence, whereas largely disjoint curated collections create rare but strong dependence among overlapping sets. Consequently, independence-based multiple-testing corrections (Bonferroni or Benjamini--Hochberg under its positive-dependence conditions) need not achieve nominal error rates; the discrepancy is driven by the database, not the method.

These are often treated as separate problems, but in CATFISH both reduce to the same object: a vector of correlated gene-level statistics. A single genome-wide null draw, carrying the estimated LD, is scored through all pathways at once. Within a pathway, it provides null components and accounts for within-pathway dependence; across pathways, it provides a null for the family minimum and accounts for between-pathway dependence. No correlation structure is estimated or modeled; both are inherited. Section~2.3 develops this.

The construction relies on three assumptions: (i) \emph{Approximate normality}: under the null, the pathway-level vector satisfies $\mathbf{Z}_S\sim\mathcal{N}(0,R_S)$. (ii) \emph{Correlation-matrix adequacy}: correlation matrices from MAGMA-reported gene pairs provide an LD-informed working approximation to the unobserved gene-level covariance; unreported pairs are set to zero, and adequacy is evaluated via calibration and robustness analyses below. (iii) \emph{No residual structural confounding}: gene-level inputs are adjusted so pathway signals are not primarily driven by gene size or SNP density.

\subsection*{2.3 Calibration and family-wise control from a shared correlated null}

CATFISH obtains two different $p$-values for every pathway: a marginal one,
which asks whether that pathway is enriched, and a family-wise one, which asks whether evidence this strong would appear anywhere among the $K$ pathways tested by chance. Both are read from a single set of resampled null draws. The procedure has four steps.

\paragraph{Step 1: generate the null.}
Gene--gene correlations are read from MAGMA's \texttt{.genes.raw} output as a list of gene pairs and their correlations. For each chromosome $c$ containing $m_c$ genes we form an $m_c\times m_c$ correlation matrix $R_c$ by starting from the identity and filling in the reported pairs,
\[
R_c = I_{m_c} + \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{P}_c}
      r_{ij}\left(e_ie_j^{\top}+e_je_i^{\top}\right),
\]
where $\mathcal{P}_c$ is the set of reported pairs for chromosome $c$; pairs not in $\mathcal{P}_c$ remain zero. We compute correlations only for gene pairs close enough to share LD, so $R_c$ is banded by construction: zeros for distant genes are approximations, not imputations, whereas zeros for nearby but unreported pairs introduce approximation error (see Section~5). Genes on different chromosomes are treated as independent, which is structurally correct. If $R_c$ is not positive definite, we floor eigenvalues at $10^{-6}$, reconstruct, and renormalise the diagonal; the implementation reports affected chromosomes.

Writing $R_c=U_c^{\top}U_c$ for the Cholesky factor, replicate $b$ draws
\[
\mathbf{Z}^{(b)}_c = U_c^{\top}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\qquad
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{m_c}),
\]
independently per chromosome, and the concatenation $\mathbf{Z}^{(b)}$ is one genome-wide null realisation carrying the estimated LD structure. Gene-level null $p$-values follow by the Gaussian copula $p_g^{(b)}=1-\Phi(Z_g^{(b)})$, matching MAGMA's one-sided sign convention. This is repeated for $b=1,\dots,B$.

We draw genome-wide, not pathway-by-pathway, because Step~4 requires all $K$ pathways to be scored on the \emph{same} realisation so shared genes perturb them jointly. Drawing each pathway from its own correlation submatrix would be marginally correct but would remove the dependence structure Step~4 needs.

\paragraph{Step 2: score every pathway on every replicate.}
For each replicate we recompute all five component statistics and the omnibus operator $\mathcal{O}(\cdot)$ for every pathway in the family $\mathcal{F}=\{S_1,\dots,S_K\}$, giving a $B\times K$ matrix of null omnibus values
\[
Q^{(b)}_k = \mathcal{O}\!\left(\mathbf{p}^{(b)}(S_k)\right),
\qquad b=1,\dots,B,\quad k=1,\dots,K .
\]
Because all components are recomputed from the same draw, $Q$ preserves within-pathway gene dependence, cross-method dependence, and—since all $K$ pathways share the draw—between-pathway dependence. The observed values $q^{\mathrm{obs}}_k=\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{obs}}(S_k))$ are computed the same way from the real data. Applying $\mathcal{O}$ to the observed components without resampling yields the uncalibrated analytic omnibus $\mathrm{Omnibus}_{\mathrm{An}}(S_k)=q^{\mathrm{obs}}_k$, which assumes gene independence and serves as the baseline.

\paragraph{Step 3: the marginal \texorpdfstring{$p$}{p}-value.}
Compare each pathway's observed statistic with its own null values across the $B$ replicates. The LD-aware marginal $p$-value is the Monte Carlo tail probability
\[
\tilde p_k \;=\;
\frac{1+\sum_{b=1}^{B}\mathbf{1}\!\left(Q^{(b)}_k\le q^{\mathrm{obs}}_k\right)}{B+1}.
\]
This quantity is computed from the genome-wide draw rather than from the pathway-specific submatrix $R_{S_k}$; because $R_{S_k}$ is simply the $R_c$ submatrix for that pathway's genes, both use the same correlation information, so nothing is lost. We call $\tilde p_k$ the per-pathway calibrated $p$-value and use it to rank pathways. It corresponds to what is often called MVN calibration; we avoid that label because the multivariate normal model is the generator of the null draws, not a separate calibration step, and the same draws serve Step~4.

\paragraph{Step 4: the family-wise \texorpdfstring{$p$}{p}-value.}
Within each replicate, record the smallest statistic across the whole family,
\[
M^{(b)} = \min_{1\le k\le K} Q^{(b)}_k ,
\]
and compare each observed statistic with the resulting distribution. This gives the Westfall--Young adjusted $p$-value \citep{Westfall1993}
\[
\tilde p^{\mathrm{WY}}_k \;=\;
\frac{1+\sum_{b=1}^{B}\mathbf{1}\!\left(M^{(b)}\le q^{\mathrm{obs}}_k\right)}{B+1},
\]
Rejecting all pathways with $\tilde p^{\mathrm{WY}}_k\le\alpha$ controls the family-wise error rate at level $\alpha$ under the complete null, whatever the dependence among pathways, because a false rejection anywhere in the family requires the family minimum to cross the threshold. Three further points apply. First, the control is in the strong sense, not only under the complete null. Westfall--Young procedures control the family-wise error rate strongly when the joint distribution of the statistics for the true null hypotheses does not depend on which other hypotheses are false---the condition of subset pivotality \citep{Westfall1993}. Two features of the construction give it. Each pathway statistic is a deterministic function of its own genes' $z$-scores only; and under the model of Step~1, with association represented as a shift in the means of the associated genes, the genes of a pathway containing no associated gene are jointly $\mathcal{N}(0,R)$, restricted to those genes, whatever happens elsewhere. The observed statistics of the null pathways therefore have exactly their complete-null joint distribution, and because the minimum over any subset of pathways is never smaller than the minimum over the whole family, the probability that any null pathway crosses the threshold is at most $\alpha$. The argument requires the soft TFisher truncation grid to be fixed in advance: a grid chosen from the empirical distribution of all gene-level $p$-values, as in data-adaptive implementations, would make every pathway's statistic depend on genes outside it. Second, the null is parametric: it is generated from the multivariate normal model of Step~1 rather than by permuting phenotypes, so the procedure is Westfall--Young in construction but inherits the adequacy of that model. Third, the counting form of the adjusted $p$-value above, in which null minima equal to the observed value count as at least as extreme, is required rather than a matter of taste. A threshold taken as a quantile of $M$ and applied with an inclusive comparison rejects every observation tied at that quantile, and if the omnibus loses resolution in the tail so that many null minima coincide, the empirical family-wise error rate can approach one (Section~4.6).

Between-pathway correlation is never explicitly estimated or modelled. Because all $K$ pathways are evaluated on the same draw in Step~2, genes shared across pathways perturb them jointly, as in the observed data, and this dependence is reflected in the distribution of $M$. The threshold $\hat t_\alpha=\mathrm{quantile}_\alpha(M)$ is the family-wise significance cutoff implied by the pathway database in use; its ratio to the Bonferroni cutoff $\alpha/K$ quantifies the database’s departure from independence at no extra cost, provided it is read on calibrated statistics; on the analytic scale the ratio also absorbs any miscalibration of the per-pathway statistic (Section~4.1).

Steps~3 and~4 use the same matrix $Q$ and require no additional resampling. Their outputs relate as a raw and adjusted $p$-value: $\tilde p_k$ assesses pathway $S_k$ alone, while $\tilde p^{\mathrm{WY}}_k$ accounts for testing $K$ pathways. Both are reported; neither replaces the other.

\paragraph{Size standardisation.}
A minimum across tests is meaningful only when the tests share a scale. The raw omnibus does not: Fisher’s statistic increases with $m$, so the Step~4 family minimum would mechanically favor the largest sets. We therefore also report a variant that first standardizes each observed value within its own column of $Q$ as a $z$-score, $z_k=(q^{\mathrm{obs}}_k-\mu_k)/\sigma_k$, where $\mu_k$ and $\sigma_k$ are that column’s mean and standard deviation, and then takes the row minimum. This rescales values for the minimum operation; it is not a pathway-size bias correction, and Section~4.1 shows it does not behave as one. In the simulations of Section~2.6 every pathway in a configuration has the same size and dependence structure, so the pathways are exchangeable, share one marginal null, and the row minimum is taken on the raw scale; standardisation is needed only when pathway sizes differ, as in real data. We standardise by location and scale rather than by each pathway's empirical null distribution function. The latter would put pathways on an exactly common scale, but would resolve each to only $1/(B+1)$, which is coarser than the family minimum requires and would reintroduce ties.

\paragraph{Competitive test.}
The Westfall--Young test above is self-contained: its null hypothesis is that no
gene in the pathway is associated with the trait. CATFISH also tests the
competitive hypothesis that the genes of a pathway are no more associated than
genes elsewhere in the same GWAS. The omnibus statistic and both readings of the
null matrix (each pathway's calibrated $p$-value and the family-wise
$p$-value from the minimum of the standardised statistics) are unchanged; only
the null replicates differ. Genes are placed in genome order (chromosome, then
start position) and joined end to end. In each replicate every gene takes the
observed score of the gene $s$ places further along this order, wrapping at the
end, for one random shift $s$ shared by all pathways, and every pathway is
rescored. A pathway's genes therefore keep their spacing, so genes in linkage
disequilibrium with one another move together, while their scores come from a
random position in the genome; the genome-wide distribution of gene scores,
including any polygenic signal, is present in the null as well as in the
observed statistic. Because one shift is applied to all pathways, overlap
between pathways is preserved within each replicate and the minimum over
pathways gives strong family-wise control. Shifts are drawn without replacement,
so at most $G-1$ replicates exist for $G$ genes. The null does not match genes
on size or SNP count. A pathway is called by both tests when
$\max(p_{\mathrm{FWER}}^{\mathrm{self}}, p_{\mathrm{FWER}}^{\mathrm{comp}})\le\alpha$,
an intersection--union test that controls the family-wise error of the joint
claim at $\alpha$ without further correction \citep{Berger1982}.

\subsection*{2.4 Numerical implementation in log space}

The omnibus statistic must be computed entirely in log space. This is a correctness requirement, not an optimisation, and it prevents silent failure.

The smallest positive normalized double is $\approx2.2\times10^{-308}$. In a GWAS with $n\approx1.3$ million, gene-level $p<10^{-300}$ is common: in the GLGC LDL-cholesterol data (Section~3.4), the published $p$-value is exactly zero for 473 chromosome~1 variants, including the \textit{SORT1} lead rs12740374 at $-\log_{10}p=1725$. Combining even a few such genes with Fisher’s method yields a pathway statistic whose double-precision $p$-value underflows to zero. All affected pathways then share the same floor value, ties are broken by storage order, and the ranking the analysis aims to produce is silently corrupted. In our first implementation, the top-ranked pathways were (on inspection) alphabetical.

Five changes are needed. (i) Compute all component statistics on $\ell_g=\log p_g$ and return log $p$-values via \texttt{stats::pchisq(\dots,\,log.p=TRUE)} and \texttt{stats::qnorm(\dots,\,log.p=TRUE)}. (ii) Evaluate the ACAT sum using the small-$p$ asymptote: for $p<10^{-15}$, use $\tan\{\pi(\tfrac12-p)\}\approx 1/(p\pi)$, accumulating those terms as a log-sum-exp of $-\ell_g-\log\pi$; use the trigonometric form for the rest. (iii) Replace $p=\tfrac12-\arctan(T)/\pi$ with $p\approx1/(T\pi)$ for $T>10^{6}$, where cancellation near $\tfrac12$ dominates truncation error. (iv) Since ACAT can be negative when most inputs exceed $0.5$, handle that case in a separate branch computing $\tfrac12-\arctan(T)/\pi$ directly; clamping would set $p=0.5$ and destroy ordering in the upper half. (v) The minP inversion $1-(1-q)^m$ underflows for $q\lesssim10^{-16}/m$, causing minP to drop out; use the $m q$ asymptote whenever $\log m+\ell_{\min}<\log 10^{-8}$.

These changes let the pipeline represent pathway $p$-values down to $e^{-745}\approx10^{-324}$ and, crucially, preserve their \emph{ordering} well below where probability-scale computations fail. They are implemented in the scripts used for the real-data analyses. The simulations of Section~2.6 instead call the component functions of the CATFISH package directly, so that they validate the code a user runs; those functions work on the probability scale with a lower floor of $10^{-300}$, which is adequate there because no simulated null approaches it. Soft TFisher is subject to its own, much higher, precision limit (Section~2.1).

\subsection*{2.5 Candidate-gene prioritization}

The output a biologist acts on is a short list of genes, so CATFISH ranks genes by a composite score over three evidence layers:
\[
\mathrm{Score}(g)=S_{\mathrm{GWAS}}(g)+S_{\mathrm{MAGMA}}(g)
+0.5\,S_{\mathrm{PATH}}(g)+0.3\,B_{\mathrm{hub}}(g).
\]
Each layer enters by rank rather than by its raw statistic,
$S_\bullet(g)=-\log_{10}\!\left(r_\bullet(g)/N_\bullet\right)$, where
$r_{\mathrm{GWAS}}$ is the gene's rank by minimum SNP-level $p$-value,
$r_{\mathrm{MAGMA}}$ its rank by gene-level $p$-value, and $r_{\mathrm{PATH}}$ the rank of its best pathway by the size-standardised Westfall--Young statistic $z_k$ of Section~2.3; rank one is the strongest evidence. We rank pathways on $z_k$ for two reasons. It is the statistic the family-wise test is computed on, so the gene score uses the same pathway ordering that is reported; and it measures each pathway against its own correlated null, whereas the analytic omnibus assumes independent genes and overstates pathways whose genes are in LD (Section~3.2). The family-wise $p$-value gives the same ordering but is floored at $1/(B+1)$, which ties the strongest pathways. The hub term $B_{\mathrm{hub}}(g)=\log_{10}(n_{\mathrm{path}}(g)+1)$ rewards membership in several pathways. Genes missing a layer score zero for it, so an absent layer neither helps nor penalises.

We use ranks instead of $-\log_{10}p$ to make layers with different tail behaviour comparable and to avoid the $p$-value floors in Section~2.4. Pathway evidence is halved because the pathway $p$-value is a set-level statistic shared across member genes; the hub term is intentionally secondary. We fixed the weights \emph{a priori} and assessed them afterwards over a grid, $w_{\mathrm{path}}\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$ and $w_{\mathrm{hub}}\in\{0,0.1,\dots,0.5\}$, in a truth-planted simulation and in the real analyses of Section~3.5 (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:weights}). The simulation draws $400$ replicate datasets of $500$ genes and $50$ pathways, plants causal genes concentrated in five pathways, and generates correlated GWAS-, MAGMA- and pathway-level evidence for them \citep{Tandukar2025Thesis}. Rankings were stable across the grid: the Spearman correlation of each full ranking with the default ranking was at least $0.986$, and the top twenty genes overlapped the default top twenty by at least $88\%$. Recovery of planted causal genes in the top twenty rose steadily with the pathway weight, from $0.71$ to $0.85$, and was $0.80$ at the defaults, so the defaults are not the optimum of that simulation. They are a deliberately conservative middle value: the pathway layer is set-level evidence shared by every member gene and, in curated databases, partly encodes prior knowledge, so it should not outweigh direct genetic evidence. We did not re-tune the weights on the validation genes of Section~3.7.

\emph{Where the gain comes from.} Two checks separate the contribution of the
pathway layer from that of the association layers. First, genes are ranked with
the pathway weight set to zero, so the score reduces to the GWAS and MAGMA
layers. Second, a gene's own association contributes to the evidence of every
pathway that contains it, so a gene could be raised by its own signal passing
through its pathway. The pathway evidence of each gene being checked (for
example, the positive controls of a trait) is therefore recomputed with that
gene left out: for each tested pathway $P$
containing gene $g$, $P$ and $P$ without $g$ are scored in one Westfall--Young
run on the same null draws, the change in $z_k$ is added to $P$'s statistic from
the full analysis, the strongest of these becomes $g$'s pathway evidence, and
its pathway rank, score and overall rank are recomputed with every other gene
unchanged (\texttt{wy\_logo.R}, \texttt{39\_logo\_recovery.R}). Reduced sets
are allowed one gene fewer than the minimum pathway size, so that removing a
gene does not drop its pathway. If the gene keeps its rank, its lift comes from
the other genes of its pathways; if it falls back to its rank without the
pathway layer, the lift was its own.

The score is a heuristic ranking metric, not an inferential model: it has no null distribution or $p$-value. We also report per-layer ranks, because the GWAS and MAGMA layers use the same variants and are not independent evidence.

\subsection*{2.6 Simulation design}

Simulations are run on the omnibus statistic under its null, except where a signal is injected deliberately (item~6). Two dependence designs are used. In the \emph{shared-universe} design, pathways are overlapping subsets of one gene universe: $G=3000$ genes in independent blocks of 50 with exchangeable within-block correlation $\rho$, standing in for chromosomes and LD blocks, from the first 600 of which we draw $K=150$ pathways of $m$ genes each at random. Each gene therefore belongs to several pathways, a pathway spans several blocks, and a single genome-wide draw moves every pathway containing a given gene---the between-pathway dependence the family-wise procedure is designed to control. In the \emph{exchangeable} design, used only in item~1, each pathway is drawn independently and all $m$ of its genes are mutually correlated at $\rho$. That design has no between-pathway dependence; it isolates within-pathway correlation at an intensity well beyond what block-structured LD produces, and serves as a stress test. Each study reports two summaries of the null matrix: per-pathway marginal type~I error (by columns) and family-wise error rate (by rows), with Bonferroni as the family-wise comparator. Thresholds are fitted on one set of draws and evaluated on a held-out set, so rates are not correct by construction. Component statistics are computed with the CATFISH package functions, using the soft TFisher grid, undefined-component rule and precision cap of Section~2.1. We do not simulate genotypes or phenotypes or run a full GWAS; that is future work (Section~5) and is not needed for the questions here. Each study corresponds to a script in \texttt{catfish2/scripts/} and writes a CSV to \texttt{catfish2/results/} from which the tables and figures are generated.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Null calibration and type~I error} (\texttt{01\_null\_calibration.R}), exchangeable design. Under the global null we compare four decision rules: the analytic independence-based $p$-value; per-pathway calibration against a null bank shared by all pathways of a given size; Bonferroni; and the Westfall--Young adjusted $p$-value of Step~4, with the null distribution of the family minimum estimated on one half of the simulated families and evaluated on the other. We vary $\rho\in\{0,0.3,0.6\}$ and $m\in\{5,20,50\}$ and report type~I error at $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.01\}$, family-wise error, the genomic-control inflation factor $\lambda=\mathrm{median}(\chi^2_1\text{-transformed }p)/0.4549$, and the number of distinct values taken by the family minimum, which detects loss of resolution directly.

\item \textbf{Component-breakage stress test} (\texttt{02\_breakage\_stress.R}).
We miscalibrate $k$ of five components by replacing each affected component’s $p$-value with $p^{\gamma}$, and test whether the omnibus and its calibration remain valid. We run both corruption directions: for $0<p<1$, $\gamma<1$ yields $p^{\gamma}>p$ (conservative) and $\gamma>1$ yields $p^{\gamma}<p$ (anti-conservative), the latter being most relevant for false positives. We use $\gamma\in\{0.7,1.4\}$; sweep $k=0,\dots,4$; evaluate up to three component combinations per $k$; and cross with the same LD regimes at $m\in\{20,50\}$. We apply corruption to both null replicates and observed statistics, allowing resampling calibration to absorb it.

\item \textbf{Leave-one-out component sensitivity} (\texttt{03\_adaptive\_loo.R}).
We remove each component in turn, recompute the null bank and the calibration from scratch, and measure the change in type~I error and $\lambda$. This isolates how much each component contributes to, or destabilizes, the calibrated omnibus, and identifies components whose removal would change inference.

\item \textbf{Per-component calibration} (\texttt{09\_component\_calibration.R}).
Rather than combining, we calibrate each of the five components against its own null and report analytic and calibrated type~I error, family-wise rate and $\lambda$ per component. This identifies which statistics LD actually breaks and whether a single calibrated component is usable in place of the omnibus when the signal architecture is known in advance.

\item \textbf{Robustness to an incomplete correlation file}
(\texttt{08\_missing\_correlation.R}). Data are simulated under the true correlation, but calibration (null bank, marginal $p$-values, and Westfall--Young threshold) uses a matrix where a fraction $\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$ of off-diagonal entries is set to zero, mimicking unreported gene pairs. Evaluation draws are shared across missingness levels; only calibration varies. For real data, \texttt{10\_pair\_coverage.R} places a correlation file on this scale by reporting the fraction of within-pathway gene pairs on the same chromosome and close enough to be correlated for which MAGMA reports a value.

\item \textbf{Archetype power} (\texttt{17\_archetype\_power\_wy.R}), shared-universe design with $\rho=0.3$. One pathway in each family carries signal shaped as one of the five archetypes of Section~3.1 (Table~\ref{tab:archetype_summary}); the remaining $K-1$ are null. Signal is a mean shift on the gene-level $z$ scale applied to a fixed fraction of the pathway's genes, at positions permuted in every replicate: SSS shifts $15\%$ of genes by $3.0$; MDS $60\%$ by $1.1$; WDE $90\%$ by $0.55$; MSA $10\%$ by $1.8$ and a further $60\%$ by $1.1$; and SGP $5\%$ by $4.0$, with at least one gene in every layer. Shifts are multiplied by an effect scale over a grid running from undetectable to saturated, at $m\in\{5,20,50\}$. For each component and for the omnibus we record the probability that the signal pathway clears its own Westfall--Young threshold. Regret is the difference in that probability between the best single component and the omnibus in the same cell.
\end{enumerate}

Unless noted, each configuration uses $B$ null draws for the calibration bank and separate replicate datasets for evaluation; both settings are defined at the top of each script, allowing a reduced smoke test via \texttt{bash run\_all.sh quick}.

\paragraph{Simulation with known truth on real linkage disequilibrium.}
We compared CATFISH with MAGMA gene-set analysis on GWAS summary statistics simulated under real LD (\texttt{scripts/hpc/simulation\_check\_catfish}), ensuring both methods used identical SNP statistics and MAGMA gene scores. Genotypes from a reference panel of $n$ individuals were restricted to selected chromosomes/SNPs and standardised to $X$ ($n\times M$). With $u=e/\sqrt{n}$ and $e\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_n)$, SNP statistics $z=X^\top u$ had covariance equal to the panel correlation matrix. 

We added signal at causal SNPs by assigning each causal SNP $k$ an expected statistic $\lambda_k$ (random sign) and propagating it to SNP $j$ within 1~Mb as $r_{jk}\lambda_k$, where $r_{jk}$ is the panel correlation; limiting propagation to local LD avoids inflation from chance long-range correlations in a finite panel. Each causal gene was assigned one causal SNP, chosen uniformly from its mapped SNPs. 

We simulated two nulls: (i) no causal SNPs; and (ii) a polygenic background where a fixed fraction of genes had one modest-effect causal SNP, with no pathway enrichment. Other scenarios added signal in one target pathway on top of this background, following the five archetypes in Table~\ref{tab:archetype_summary}: a specified fraction of pathway genes was made causal, with $|\lambda|=\delta$ (archetype effect) times a shared effect scale. Background genes were sampled outside the target pathway. Target pathways (three prespecified sizes) were chosen from pathways unrelated to the mimicked trait. Each replicate was analysed with MAGMA gene analysis (SNP-wise mean), CATFISH (self-contained, competitive, and both), MAGMA gene-set analysis (competitive and self-contained via \texttt{--model self-contained}), and a two-stage ACAT test \citep{Liu2019ACATV,Liu2020ACAT}: SNP $p$-values within each gene were combined by the Cauchy method to form gene $p$-values, which were then combined (equal weights) to obtain pathway $p$-values. ACAT is self-contained and requires no LD correction because the Cauchy combination is tail-valid under dependence. CATFISH used Westfall--Young FWER control at $p\le0.05$; MAGMA and ACAT used Bonferroni, $p\le0.05/K$ over $K$ tested pathways. Power was the fraction of replicates in which the target pathway was called. An error was calling a pathway with no genes within 1~Mb of any target causal SNP; with no target pathway, every call was an error. Family-wise error was the fraction of replicates with at least one error. Under a polygenic background, self-contained tests are expected to call pathways containing truly associated background genes, so errors were assessed for competitive tests and the joint call only.

Simulations used the 1000 Genomes EUR lipid panel (503 individuals), chromosomes 1–10, and HapMap3 SNPs with MAF $\ge0.01$ (748,822 SNPs). Genes were annotated with MAGMA ±10 kb windows (9,645 genes); Reactome was restricted to these genes, yielding K=1,021 pathways (10–200 genes). The background set 2% of genes as causal with |λ|=3. An effect scale of 3 was chosen from a pilot (2, 4, 6) to avoid no detection or saturation. Target pathways contained 13, 36, and 97 genes. The 17 scenario–size combinations had 50 replicates each (850 total). CATFISH used B=2,000 null replicates per test, five components with τ∈{0.01,0.05,0.5}, and gene-level genomic control.

\subsection*{2.7 Real-data analyses}

\textbf{Maize grain nitrogen.} Gene-level statistics were computed with MAGMA
Used MLM GWAS results for grain nitrogen content, assigning SNPs to genes within $\pm 10\,\mathrm{kb}$. Pathways were from CornCyc \citep{Hawkins2021CornCyc,Caspi2008BioCyc}, yielding $172$ pathways after size filtering. Gene--gene correlations were extracted per chromosome from MAGMA \texttt{.genes.raw}; we confirmed all ten chromosomes were included after an earlier parsing error captured only chromosome~1 and treated $85\%$ of the genome as independent. Candidate genes were scored with the three-layer method (Section~2.5) across all $34{,}873$ annotated genes.

\textbf{Human LDL and HDL cholesterol.} We used the Global Lipids Genetics
Consortium LDL- and HDL-cholesterol summary statistics from the European-ancestry meta-analysis ($n\approx1.3$ million) \citep{Graham2021GLGC}. Both traits were processed identically, as described below.

\emph{Variant filtering and the $p$-value floor.} Variants were restricted to
rsIDs are from the 1000 Genomes European LD reference panel. The released \texttt{pvalue} column underflows to zero at top loci, so $p$-values were reconstructed from \texttt{pvalue\_neg\_log10}. It therefore uses an unfloored $-\log_{10}p$ file produced by \texttt{11\_munge\_neglog10.sh}.

\emph{Gene analysis.} Variants were mapped to genes using NCBI37.3 locations with a $10\,\mathrm{kb}$ upstream/downstream window. MAGMA was run per chromosome with \texttt{--batch} and combined using \texttt{--merge} to retain gene--gene correlations in the merged \texttt{.genes.raw} file; concatenating per-chromosome outputs would lose these correlations.

\emph{Pathways.} Pathway inference used Reactome from MSigDB C2:CP ($1{,}390$ sets after size filtering); HumanCyc was also converted to CATFISH format for comparison. We report Reactome because its hierarchical structure induces strong between-pathway dependence, the setting targeted by the family-wise procedure.

\emph{Candidate genes.} The GWAS layer uses MAGMA's own
We used the \texttt{.genes.annot} file rather than re-deriving a window, ensuring the GWAS and gene layers use identical variant sets. \emph{Positive controls.} Thirteen established LDL-C genes (\textit{LDLR}, \textit{PCSK9}, \textit{APOB}, \textit{APOE}, \textit{SORT1}, \textit{HMGCR}, \textit{NPC1L1}, \textit{ABCG8}, \textit{LPA}, \textit{CETP}, \textit{LDLRAP1}, \textit{MYLIP}, \textit{ANGPTL3}) and twelve established HDL-C genes (\textit{CETP}, \textit{LIPC}, \textit{LIPG}, \textit{ABCA1}, \textit{APOA1}, \textit{APOA5}, \textit{LCAT}, \textit{SCARB1}, \textit{PLTP}, \textit{LPL}, \textit{ANGPTL4}, \textit{CUBN}) were specified before prioritization and used only for assessment. With \texttt{--model self-contained}, MAGMA instead reports a self-contained test matching CATFISH’s question. We restricted results to the $1{,}390$ pathways tested by CATFISH and applied Bonferroni correction across these sets, since MAGMA provides no family-wise procedure. MAGMA uses its internally computed gene statistics without the centring described below.

\textbf{Sorghum dry biomass.} We analysed dry biomass yield (dry tons per acre) in the sorghum Bioenergy Association Panel (BAP) \citep{Brenton2016}: $327$ lines with measurements. Each permuted phenotype was run through the full analysis pipeline: GEMMA mixed model, MAGMA per chromosome with the same windows and SNP-wise mean model, and Westfall--Young calibration with the same pathways and settings. Because MAGMA gene--gene correlations depend only on genotypes, one correlation file was used for all permutations. Since every pathway is null in every permutation, calibrated $p$-values should be uniform, and a family-wise $p \le 0.05$ should occur in at most $5\%$ of permutations.

\textbf{Sorghum stem composition.} To assess whether the gene score captures new biology (not just calibration), we analysed three stem-composition traits in the same panel \citep{Brenton2016}: Brix at physiological maturity (stem-juice soluble solids; $\nBapNBrix$ lines) and two dry-matter fractions, neutral detergent fibre (NDF; cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin; $\nBapNNDF$ lines) and non-fibre carbohydrate (NFC; mainly sugars and starch; $\nBapNNFC$ lines). Each trait was tested with GEMMA’s univariate linear mixed model \citep{Zhou2012GEMMA} (Wald test) via vcf2gwas v0.8.9 \citep{Vogt2022vcf2gwas} on the panel genotypes ($\nBapNlines$ lines), using the centred relatedness matrix (all chromosomes), the first three genotype PCs as covariates, and variants with minor allele frequency $\ge0.05$. Gene analysis, pathways, Westfall--Young calibration (self-contained and competitive), genomic control and the gene score matched the dry-biomass analysis: MAGMA SNP-wise mean per chromosome with $25\,\mathrm{kb}$ windows and the same correlation structure, SorghumBicolorCyc pathways (5--200 genes), $B=10{,}000$, and the same seed (\texttt{hpc/bap\_gwas/01}--\texttt{04}). Genes were ranked by gene score per trait, and we identified genes in the top 100 for all three traits. Ranks are reported among all genes and, as for dry biomass, among genes with evidence in all three layers.

\emph{The chromosome~4 locus.} For the shared locus (Section~3.7), the lead variant for each trait was the strongest signal between $63.85$ and $64.10\,\mathrm{Mb}$. Linkage disequilibrium ($r^2$) between lead variants was computed from panel genotypes using PLINK~1.9 \citep{Chang2015PLINK}. Allele effects at the Brix lead variant were taken from each trait’s mixed model and reported in trait SD units. To test whether the allele’s effect on fibre reflects sugar–fibre correlation, NDF was regressed on allele count across lines with and without Brix as a covariate. This regression is descriptive and ignores relatedness; association evidence comes from the mixed model. Code: \texttt{hpc/bap\_gwas/05\_chr4\_locus\_LSF.sh}, \texttt{43\_bap\_chr4\_locus.R}, \texttt{44\_bap\_chr4\_numbers.R}.

\emph{Leave one gene out.} To measure how much of each candidate's rank comes
from its own association passing through its pathways, the leave-one-gene-out
check of Section~2.5 was applied to every gene with pathway evidence among the
top $100$ of each trait's gene score ($\nBapLogoNBrix$, $\nBapLogoNNDF$ and
$\nBapLogoNNFC$ genes for Brix, NDF and NFC), which include \textit{SbALDH3E1} and the thioredoxin reductase. For
each such gene and each tested pathway containing it, the pathway was scored
with and without the gene in one Westfall--Young run with the settings of the
trait's pathway analysis (gene-level genomic control, the trait's gene--gene
correlations, SorghumBicolorCyc, $B=10{,}000$, the same truncation grid and
seed; reduced sets of at least $4$ genes). Each gene's pathway evidence, pathway
score, gene score and rank were then recomputed with every other gene unchanged,
both among all genes and among the genes with evidence in all three layers. We
report each candidate's rank without the pathway layer, with it, and without
its own contribution, and, for all checked genes of a trait, the number that
stay in the top $100$ and their median ranks
(\texttt{hpc/bap\_gwas/06\_logo\_LSF.sh}, \texttt{47\_bap\_logo.R}).

\textbf{Gene-level genomic control.} For all real analyses (ten lipid trait--ancestry sets, sorghum dry biomass, and three stem-composition traits), we centred gene $p$-values before pathway testing. We converted each gene $p$-value to a $z$-score $z_g=\Phi^{-1}(1-p_g)$, computed from $\log p_g$ to preserve ordering below $10^{-300}$ (Section~2.4), subtracted the GWAS-wide median $\hat\delta$, and converted back: $p^{\mathrm{GC}}_g=1-\Phi(z_g-\hat\delta)$. This location genomic control \citep{Devlin1999} uses one constant per GWAS, preserves gene order, gene--gene correlations, and the null draws of Section~2.3, and sets the median gene $p$-value to $0.5$. We included it because permutation nulls showed GWAS-specific shifts in gene $z$-scores that a self-contained test can misread as pathway signal, while the correlated null lacks this shift (Section~3.6). If no shift is present, centring has no effect; if the GWAS is saturated, centring removes some true polygenic signal and effectively asks whether pathway genes are more associated than the genome-wide median gene (Section~4.2). The estimated shifts were $0.15$--$0.33$ in the eight non-European lipid sets, $1.38$ (LDL-C) and $1.77$ (HDL-C) in the European sets, $0.13$ for sorghum dry biomass, and $\nBapGCBrix$, $\nBapGCNDF$ and $\nBapGCNFC$ for Brix, NDF and NFC.

\textbf{Competitive test and the gene score.} For each real analysis, we ran the competitive test (Section~2.3) alongside the self-contained test on the same centred gene $p$-values and pathways, using $B=10{,}000$ circular shifts (\texttt{wy\_gc\_real.R} with \texttt{COMP=1}). To assess the impact on candidate genes, we recomputed the gene score (Section~2.5) with the pathway layer ranked by the competitive standardised statistic $z_k^{\mathrm{comp}}$ or by the joint statistic $\max(z_k, z_k^{\mathrm{comp}})$ (the weaker of the two) instead of $z_k$, keeping all other layers, weights, and pathway sets unchanged (\texttt{36\_prioritize\_comp.R}). We then compared positive-control recovery across the three versions (\texttt{37\_prioritize\_comp\_recovery.R}).

\textbf{Comparison with PoPS.} We compared our gene score with PoPS v0.2 \citep{Weeks2023PoPS} using European LDL-C and HDL-C data. PoPS was run with default settings (ridge regression on public gene features; MAGMA covariates and error covariance; feature selection at $p<0.05$; HLA removed) on MAGMA gene statistics computed as above (same reference panel, $\pm10$~kb window, SNP-wise mean model) but using the required Ensembl annotation ($18{,}383$ protein-coding genes). Known genes were Open Targets Genetics gold-standard genes \citep{Mountjoy2021L2G} from the 2019 and 2023 releases (LDL-C: LDL cholesterol measurement and hypercholesterolaemia; HDL-C: HDL cholesterol measurement): $15$ for LDL-C and $18$ for HDL-C, curated independently of this study. For genes scored by both methods ($17{,}736$; CATFISH genes matched to Ensembl by unique symbol), we ranked genes by PoPS, CATFISH, best SNP $p$-value, and MAGMA gene $p$-value, and counted known genes in the top $10$, $20$, $50$, $100$, and $200$. We also evaluated single loci: for each gold-standard lead variant, candidates were genes with a transcription start site within $500$~kb; a locus was “solved” if the top-ranked candidate was the gold-standard gene (\texttt{40\_gold\_standard\_lipids.py}, \texttt{41\_benchmark\_genes.R}, \texttt{42\_benchmark\_topk.R}).

\textbf{Benchmark across nine traits.} To evaluate the gene score beyond lipids, we added seven European GWAS with Open Targets gold-standard genes \citep{Mountjoy2021L2G} and GWAS Catalog harmonised summary statistics: coronary artery disease \citep{vanderHarst2018CAD}, breast cancer \citep{Michailidou2017BRCA}, Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis \citep{deLange2017IBD}, atrial fibrillation \citep{Nielsen2018AF}, systemic lupus erythematosus \citep{Bentham2015SLE}, and UK Biobank asthma \citep{Valette2021Asthma}. Variants were limited to 1000 Genomes European rsIDs; per-variant sample sizes were used when available, otherwise the study total. MAGMA, CATFISH (self-contained test with gene-level genomic control; Reactome), and the gene score were run as for European lipid traits, and PoPS as above (\texttt{hpc/benchmark/01}--\texttt{04}, \texttt{hpc/pops}). We excluded type 2 diabetes (only a subset of the large European meta-analysis variants are in the GWAS Catalog) and schizophrenia and rheumatoid arthritis (no European genome-wide file). Known genes were each trait’s Open Targets gold-standard genes from the 2019 and 2023 releases (LDL-C and HDL-C as above). We compared six rankings: CATFISH; CATFISH without the pathway layer (pathway and hub weights $0$; GWAS+MAGMA); best variant; MAGMA gene $p$-value; PoPS; and the mean CATFISH/PoPS rank. Rankings were computed within genes scored by both CATFISH and PoPS, and we counted known genes in the top $10$--$200$. The main analysis excluded the extended MHC (chr6, $25$--$34$~Mb, GRCh37) because PoPS excludes HLA by default and long-range LD dominates immune-trait rankings. We also report results for all genes and for genes in a tested pathway (\texttt{45\_benchmark\_topk\_all.R}).

\section{3 Results}

\subsection*{3.1 Each signal type is detected by the test built for it, and the omnibus stays close to the best test}

CATFISH scores each pathway with five component tests, combines them into one omnibus statistic, and calibrates the per-pathway and family-wise $p$-values on one set of correlated null draws (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}a; Algorithm~\ref{alg:catfish}; Section~2.3). Under the alternative, a pathway carries excess gene-level evidence, but that evidence can be spread across its genes in many ways. We define five signal types by the shape of the ranked gene-level profile (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}b; Table~\ref{tab:archetype_summary}; Supplementary Note~S1). Strong sparse signal (SSS) is expected when a committed-step enzyme, rate-limiting regulator or essential transporter controls pathway output \citep{MorenoSanchez2008,Naz2023,Tanner2018,Song2020}, and single-gene proxy (SGP) is its limiting case, a pathway that is effectively one gene. Moderate dense signal (MDS) is expected when output depends on many interacting components, as in cytokine signalling to NF-$\kappa$B \citep{Jiang2021CytoSig,Verstrepen2008NFkB}; weak diffuse enrichment (WDE) reflects a highly polygenic trait such as height \citep{Yengo2022HeightSaturation}; and mixed signal architecture (MSA) combines a few large-effect genes with a broader set of modest ones, as in LDL-cholesterol metabolism (Section~3.4) \citep{Chasman2009LipoproteinProfiles,Peloso2022WGSbloodlipids}. Real pathways may fall between types, and the type of a real pathway is not known in advance.

The mapping from signal type to component is a prediction that only simulation can test. We embedded one pathway of each type among $149$ null pathways drawn from the same correlated genes, scaled the effect from undetectable to saturated, and recorded how often it passed the family-wise Westfall--Young threshold (Section~2.6, item~6). In $\nArchCellsIn$ of $\nArchCellsTot$ type-by-size cells, the component with the highest family-wise power was one of those predicted (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}c; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:archverify}). Diffuse enrichment favoured Stouffer, with the extreme-value tests near null; sparse and single-gene signals favoured minP and ACAT, with Fisher and Stouffer nearly powerless; and dense or mixed signals favoured Fisher, with soft TFisher close behind. No component was best everywhere. Across the $\nArchInfCells$ informative cells, Fisher placed first $\nFisherFirst$ times but fourth or worse $\nFisherFourthPlus$ times, minP finished last $\nMinPLast$ times, and soft TFisher won only $\nTFFirst$ cell but never placed below third.

Because no component dominates and the type is unknown, CATFISH reports every component and combines them with an ACAT-type operator. We measured the cost of combining as \emph{regret}, the gap in power between the best component and the omnibus in a cell. Mean regret ranged from $\nArchRegretBest$ for $\nArchRegretBestA$ to $\nArchRegretWorst$ for $\nArchRegretWorstA$ (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}d; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:archregret}), while the gap between the best and worst component in the same cells often exceeded $0.5$. Choosing one component in advance risks the full gap; the omnibus risks only the regret. The exception is the single-gene type, where the omnibus lost up to $\nArchMaxRegret$ in one cell: only minP and ACAT detect such a pathway, and averaging dilutes them. This is a property of the combination, not of any component, and such a pathway is better treated as a gene-level result.

\subsection*{3.2 The shared correlated null controls per-pathway and family-wise error, and CATFISH is more powerful than MAGMA for sparse signal}

\emph{Type I error.} The analytic omnibus $p$-value assumes that the genes in a pathway are independent. To isolate this assumption, we simulated pathways that are independent of one another, with the $m$ genes of each sharing correlation $\rho\in\{0,0.3,0.6\}$, $m\in\{5,20,50\}$ and $500$ families per setting (Section~2.6, item~1). We compared the analytic $p$-value, the CATFISH per-pathway $p$-value and Bonferroni on the analytic $p$-value with the CATFISH family-wise $p$-value; the per-pathway and family-wise $p$-values come from the same null draws (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}a; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:null_calibration}). The analytic type I error was $\nTAnInd$ at $\rho=0$, $m=5$, near the nominal $0.05$, and rose with $\rho$ and $m$ to $\nTAnStr$ at $\rho=0.6$, $m=50$: correlated genes carry less independent information than the independence reference assumes. The CATFISH per-pathway $p$-value stayed within $[\nTMvnMin,\nTMvnMax]$ across the grid, and at $\alpha=0.01$ the analytic rate reached $\nTAnMaxOne$ against $\nTMvnMaxOne$. The family-wise $p$-value was tested out of sample (the null of the family minimum estimated on half the families and applied to the other half): its family-wise error ranged from $\nTWyMin$ to $\nTWyMax$, within Monte Carlo error for $250$ held-out families (standard error about $0.014$). Bonferroni inherited the analytic miscalibration, reaching $\nTBonfMod$ at $\rho=0.3$, $m=20$ and $\nTBonfStr$ at $\rho=0.6$, $m=50$; with independent pathways, all of this inflation comes from the per-pathway statistic.

\emph{The inflation factor points the wrong way.} For the analytic omnibus, $\lambda$ fell from $\nLamAnInd$ at $\rho=0$, $m=50$ to $\nLamAnStr$ at $\rho=0.6$, $m=50$, in the setting where its type I error was largest (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}b). $\lambda$ is set by the median null $p$-value and type I error by the lower tail; under strong within-pathway correlation the analytic null gains mass in the lower tail while its median rises above one half. For the CATFISH per-pathway $p$-value, $\lambda$ lay in $[\nLamMvnMin,\nLamMvnMax]$. A $\lambda$ near or below one is therefore not evidence that the tail is calibrated.

\emph{Known truth on real linkage disequilibrium.} We then simulated GWAS on real genotypes, with known enriched pathways, and compared CATFISH with MAGMA's gene-set analysis and a two-stage ACAT test (Section~2.6; $50$ replicates per setting). With no signal, CATFISH's self-contained test made no false call in $50$ replicates, against $8$ for MAGMA (family-wise error $0.16$, $95\%$ CI $0.07$--$0.29$) and $1$ for two-stage ACAT (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}c; Table~\ref{tab:sim_compare}). Both competitive tests erred in $0.06$ of replicates and the joint call in none. With only a polygenic background, under which competitive tests should make no call, CATFISH's competitive test and the joint call erred in $4/50$ ($0.08$, $95\%$ CI $0.02$--$0.19$) and MAGMA's in none.

For the self-contained question, CATFISH was more powerful than MAGMA when a few genes carried the signal (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}d). It detected a strong sparse pathway in $98\%$ of replicates against $34\%$ for MAGMA, and a single-gene pathway in $92$--$100\%$ across sizes against $0\%$, $18\%$ and $78\%$. The two were similar for dense and mixed signal, and MAGMA was slightly better for weak diffuse signal at medium size ($76\%$ against $68\%$). Two-stage ACAT matched CATFISH when one or a few genes carried the signal ($100\%$ for SSS and SGP) but lost power when the signal was spread: $2$--$28\%$ for WDE against $12$--$100\%$ for CATFISH. For the competitive question the pattern held (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}e). CATFISH called a strong sparse pathway in $66\%$, $76\%$ and $100\%$ of replicates for small, medium and large pathways, against $4\%$, $12\%$ and $36\%$ for MAGMA; for dense, diffuse and mixed signal the two were similar, MAGMA leading for small pathways (MDS $36\%$ against $20\%$, MSA $50\%$ against $42\%$). Neither competitive test called a single-gene pathway in more than $4\%$ of replicates, as expected, since one gene does not set a pathway apart from the genome; this is the question the self-contained test answers.

\subsection*{3.3 Calibration holds when components are corrupted or removed, and degrades predictably with an incomplete correlation file}

An omnibus is useful only if a misbehaving component cannot dominate it, and family-wise control only if that robustness holds at the family level. We tested both on overlapping pathways, in which each gene belongs to $\nBrkMembership$ pathways on average.

\emph{Corrupted components.} We replaced the $p$-value of $k$ of the five components with $p^{\gamma}$, anti-conservative at $\gamma=1.4$ and conservative at $\gamma=0.7$ (Figure~\ref{fig:stress}a,b; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:breakage}). With four components corrupted anti-conservatively, the analytic omnibus rose from $\nBrkAnKzero$ to $\nBrkAnKfour$ and Bonferroni from $\nBrkBonfKzero$ to $\nBrkBonfKfour$, while the CATFISH per-pathway and family-wise rates stayed at $\nBrkMvnKfour$ and $\nBrkWyKfour$. Conservative corruption moved the analytic omnibus to $\nBrkConsAnKfour$, a loss of power, with the CATFISH rates at $\nBrkConsMvnKfour$ and $\nBrkConsWyKfour$. Across all settings the family-wise rate stayed within $[\nBrkWyRangeLo,\nBrkWyRangeHi]$ and the per-pathway rate within $[\nBrkMvnRangeLo,\nBrkMvnRangeHi]$. The null draws receive the same corruption as the data, so a uniformly miscalibrated component shifts both together; a component whose error depended on the data would not cancel, and we did not test that case.

\emph{Removed components.} Dropping each component in turn and rebuilding the null draws and thresholds kept the per-pathway type I error within $[\nLooMvnMin,\nLooMvnMax]$ (full omnibus $\nLooFullMvn$) and the family-wise rate within $[\nLooWyMin,\nLooWyMax]$ (full $\nLooFullWy$; Figure~\ref{fig:stress}c; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:loo}). Averaged over sizes and variants, the Westfall--Young rate stayed at nominal across LD levels ($\nLooWyInd$, $\nLooWyMod$, $\nLooWyStr$), while Bonferroni rose from $\nLooBonfInd$ to $\nLooBonfMod$ to $\nLooBonfStr$, because it corrects an analytic $p$-value that is anti-conservative under LD (Section~3.2). Dropping a component changes sensitivity, not validity, so a user can drop one they distrust, at a cost in power for the signal type it serves. We run all five by default.

\emph{Which components LD inflates.} Calibrating the components separately showed that the damage from LD falls on two of the five, as predicted by what each measures (Figure~\ref{fig:stress}d; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:components_cal}). Under the independent null all were correctly sized; under strong LD, the aggregation statistics Fisher ($\nCmpFisherStr$) and Stouffer ($\nCmpStoufferStr$) were inflated, while the extreme-value statistics ACAT ($\nCmpACATStr$) and minP ($\nCmpminPStr$) changed little, and the analytic omnibus inherited the inflation ($\nCmpOmnibusStr$). Aggregation counts correlated genes as repeated evidence; extreme-value tests depend on the strongest gene and ignore near-duplicates. Calibration against the correlated null removed the inflation for every component (Fisher $\nCmpFisherCal$, Stouffer $\nCmpStoufferCal$, omnibus $\nCmpOmnibusCal$). When soft TFisher is undefined because no gene falls below any truncation threshold, it must be left out of the omnibus rather than set to $p=1$: ACAT treats $p=1$ as evidence against the pathway, which lowers $\lambda$ below one while inflating the tail.

\emph{An incomplete correlation file.} The correlation matrix is built from MAGMA-reported gene pairs, and unreported pairs are treated as independent. We generated data under the true correlations and calibrated with matrices in which a fraction of pairs was set to zero (Figure~\ref{fig:stress}e,f; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:missing}). With the complete matrix, the per-pathway type I error was $\nMissMvnZero$ and the family-wise error $\nMissWyZero$; with half the pairs removed, $\nMissMvnHalf$ and $\nMissWyHalf$; with all removed, $\nMissMvnFull$ and $\nMissWyFull$ under moderate LD. Relative to its nominal level, the family-wise rate was more sensitive ($\nMissRatioWyHalf\times$ nominal at $50\%$ missing, against $\nMissRatioMvnHalf\times$ for the per-pathway rate), because the family minimum is a tail statistic. It nonetheless stayed below Bonferroni ($\nMissBonfMod$ and $\nMissBonfStr$) up to $\nMissCrossover\%$ missing. The quantity to check is the fraction of nearby within-pathway gene pairs with a reported correlation. Across the sweep $\lambda$ ranged from $\nMissLamLo$ to $\nMissLamHi$ and fell below one as information was removed, the second setting in which $\lambda\le1$ accompanied an inflated tail.

\subsection*{3.4 In human blood lipids, five ancestry groups reach the same leading pathway}

We analysed LDL-C and HDL-C from the GLGC 2021 meta-analyses \citep{Graham2021GLGC} in five ancestry groups: European (EUR, $n\approx1.32$ million), African (AFR, $n\approx99{,}400$), East Asian (EAS, $n\approx146{,}500$), South Asian (SAS, $n\approx41{,}000$) and Hispanic (HIS, $n\approx48{,}100$). The ten trait--ancestry sets are independent meta-analyses with their own allele frequencies and LD, and were processed identically with pre-specified settings ($18{,}429$--$18{,}563$ genes, $1{,}390$ Reactome pathways, $B=10{,}000$, gene-level genomic control; Section~2.7).

\emph{Pathways.} In the European data, significance no longer separates pathways: $44.1\%$ of genes had $p<0.05$ for LDL-C and $11.2\%$ were genome-wide significant ($52.7\%$ and $14.8\%$ for HDL-C; Figure~\ref{fig:lipids}a,b), so a random set of 50 genes holds on average $5.6$ and $7.4$ genome-wide significant genes. Accordingly, $\nLipSigLDL$ (LDL-C) and $\nLipSigHDL$ (HDL-C) of the $\nLipNpw$ pathways had a family-wise $p\le0.05$, and large sets ranked high because they are large (size--evidence Spearman $\nLipSizeRhoLDL$ and $\nLipSizeRhoHDL$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:density}). The ranking still identified the biology. Plasma lipoprotein assembly, remodeling and clearance ranked first of $1{,}390$ in all ten sets (Figure~\ref{fig:lipids}c,d), and in every non-European set five or six of the nine pathways of the plasma-lipoprotein subtree placed in the top ten, with two LXR-mediated pathways that regulate \textit{ABCA1} close behind. In Europeans, the smaller lipoprotein sets ranked behind large generic sets but were extreme for their size (Figure~\ref{fig:lipids}e,f): among pathways within a factor of $1.25$ in size, their median percentile was $\nLipFamPctMedLDL\%$ (LDL-C) and $\nLipFamPctMedHDL\%$ (HDL-C), against $\nLipAllPctMedLipoFreeLDL\%$ and $\nLipAllPctMedLipoFreeHDL\%$ for all other pathways (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:lipid_pathways}). The same pathway ranked first in saturated and sparse genomes across five populations.

The two traits reach this pathway from different genes. Their gene-level $Z$ statistics correlated only $\nLipGeneRho$ and shared 13 of their top 100 genes (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:supp_lipid}c). Within the pathway, LDL-C is carried by receptor-mediated uptake (\textit{LDLR}, \textit{PCSK9}, \textit{LDLRAP1}, \textit{APOB}, \textit{APOE}) and HDL-C by reverse transport and lipolysis (\textit{ABCA1}, \textit{LIPC}, \textit{SCARB1}, \textit{LPL}, \textit{CETP}), with four of the twelve leading genes shared (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:supp_lipid}d). Pathway ranks of the two traits were correlated ($\nLipCrossRho$; Figure~\ref{fig:lipids}g), but this mostly reflects the shared size trend.

\emph{Self-contained and competitive tests.} MAGMA's self-contained test on the same gene statistics and pathways called even more pathways in Europeans ($1{,}358$ and $1{,}383$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:magma_compare}), so saturation is a property of the self-contained hypothesis, not of CATFISH. The two self-contained rankings agreed (Spearman $0.70$--$0.94$), but MAGMA depended more on pathway size ($0.85$ and $0.86$ in Europeans, against $0.72$ and $0.73$ for CATFISH), and ranked the lipoprotein pathway first in six sets and second in four. Competitive tests did not saturate. CATFISH's competitive test called $3$--$14$ pathways per set and MAGMA's $2$--$12$, with no dependence on size (Spearman $-0.13$ to $0.22$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:competitive_real}); every CATFISH competitive call was also a self-contained call. In Europeans, the competitive calls were mostly lipid pathways: for LDL-C, six sets of the plasma-lipoprotein subtree, both LXR-regulated sets and interferon-$\gamma$ signalling. The self-contained test asks whether a pathway carries signal, even from one gene; the competitive test asks which pathways stand out from the rest of the genome. For candidate genes the choice mattered little: ranking pathways by the competitive or joint statistic moved the median rank of the established genes by no more than seven places in any set.

\subsection*{3.5 Pathway evidence improves candidate genes, within a locus and across nine traits}

The gene score combines each gene's strongest variant, its MAGMA $p$-value and the evidence of its strongest pathway (Section~2.5). In Europeans it ranked \textit{LDLR} first for LDL-C and \textit{ABCA1} first for HDL-C (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:lipid_candidates}), and it ranked the pre-specified established genes higher than either association layer alone (Figure~\ref{fig:genes}a,b). Seven genes for LDL-C and eight for HDL-C ranked in the top twenty in all five groups (Figure~\ref{fig:genes}c,d): established \textit{LDLR}, \textit{APOE}, \textit{PCSK9} and \textit{APOB}, with \textit{APOC1}, \textit{PVRL2} and \textit{TOMM40}, for LDL-C; and established \textit{ABCA1}, \textit{CETP}, \textit{LPL}, \textit{LIPC} and \textit{APOA5}, with \textit{NLRC5}, \textit{SLC12A3} and \textit{HERPUD1}, for HDL-C. The non-established genes lie beside established ones, in the chromosome~19 apolipoprotein cluster or near \textit{CETP}, and rank high because they share the lead variant (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:replication_genes}).

Within such a locus, association cannot choose a gene, but pathway membership often can. \textit{APOE}, \textit{APOC1} and \textit{TOMM40} share their best variant in every set, so the GWAS layer ranks them equally; \textit{APOE} and \textit{APOC1} are in the leading pathway and \textit{TOMM40} and \textit{PVRL2} are not. The gene score ranked \textit{APOE} above \textit{TOMM40} in nine of the ten sets, mostly by moving \textit{TOMM40} and \textit{PVRL2} down rather than \textit{APOE} up (Figure~\ref{fig:genes}e; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:apoe_locus}), and ranked \textit{CETP} above \textit{HERPUD1} and \textit{SLC12A3} in all groups. Genes tied on all three layers cannot be separated: \textit{APOC1} shares both variant and pathway with \textit{APOE} and ranks just behind it.

The lift comes from the other genes of a pathway, not from a gene's own signal returned through it. Without the pathway layer, the established genes had median ranks of $8.5$--$267$ across the ten sets; with it, $7$--$85$, with more of them in the top 20 in every set (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:logo_recovery}). When each gene's pathway evidence was recomputed without that gene (Section~2.5), median ranks changed little ($7.5$--$102$), and in $97$ of $125$ gene--set pairs the gene still ranked higher than without pathway evidence. This does not show that the score discovers lipid biology: the established genes and the curated pathways come from the same literature, and leaving a gene out removes its own signal but not that shared knowledge. Weight sensitivity is shown in Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:weights}.

\emph{Nine traits.} To test whether pathway evidence helps beyond lipids, we added seven European GWAS with independently curated genes (Section~2.7): coronary artery disease, breast cancer, atrial fibrillation, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, systemic lupus erythematosus and asthma, giving $\nBenchNtraits$ traits and $\nBenchNgold$ Open Targets gold-standard genes, with the extended MHC excluded. The leading pathways matched each trait: plasma lipoprotein metabolism for coronary artery disease, SUMOylation and homology-directed repair for breast cancer, muscle contraction and cardiac conduction for atrial fibrillation, interferon-$\gamma$ signalling for the three autoimmune traits, and T-cell and interleukin-1 family signalling for asthma. Without the pathway layer, the genome-wide top 20, 50 and 100 held $\nBenchNpTwenty$, $\nBenchNpFifty$ and $\nBenchNpHundred$ gold-standard genes; with it, $\nBenchCatTwenty$, $\nBenchCatFifty$ and $\nBenchCatHundred$ ($\nBenchGainTwenty\%$, $\nBenchGainFifty\%$ and $\nBenchGainHundred\%$ more; Figure~\ref{fig:genes}f; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:pathway_gain_all}). The full score matched or exceeded the score without pathways in all nine traits at every cutoff, and exceeded it at one or more cutoffs in every trait. The best variant alone recovered $\nBenchGwTwenty$, $\nBenchGwFifty$ and $\nBenchGwHundred$, and the MAGMA $p$-value alone $\nBenchMgTwenty$, $\nBenchMgFifty$ and $\nBenchMgHundred$.

With its pathway layer, CATFISH matched PoPS \citep{Weeks2023PoPS}: $\nBenchCatTwenty$ against $\nBenchPopsTwenty$ gold-standard genes in the top 20, $\nBenchCatFifty$ against $\nBenchPopsFifty$ in the top 50 and $\nBenchCatHundred$ against $\nBenchPopsHundred$ in the top 100 (Figure~\ref{fig:genes}g; Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_topk_all}). CATFISH led or tied for LDL-C, HDL-C, coronary artery disease, breast cancer and atrial fibrillation, traits in which curated metabolism, signalling and ion-transport pathways are informative; PoPS led for the four immune traits, where its gene features, including immune-cell expression, capture genes that pathway membership does not. CATFISH reaches this without training a model or using features beyond pathways. Including the MHC changed little (top 20: $\nBenchAllCatTwenty$ against $\nBenchAllPopsTwenty$), and restricting to genes in a tested pathway, which favours CATFISH, gave $\nBenchAnnCatTwenty$ against $\nBenchAnnPopsTwenty$ (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:supp_bench}). The two methods find different genes (Figure~\ref{fig:genes}h): of the gold-standard genes in either top 20, $\nBenchBothTwenty$ were in both, $\nBenchOnlyCatTwenty$ only in CATFISH's and $\nBenchOnlyPopsTwenty$ only in PoPS's, and averaging the two ranks placed $\nBenchCombTwenty$, $\nBenchCombFifty$ and $\nBenchCombHundred$ in the top 20, 50 and 100, more than either alone. The leave-one-gene-out check has been run for the lipids but not yet for the other seven traits.

\subsection*{3.6 In sorghum, a permutation null tests calibration on real genotypes}

The sorghum Bioenergy Association Panel (BAP; $327$ lines) differs from the lipid data in sparse signal and long-range LD: each gene has on average $\nSorNeighbours$ neighbours with $|r|\ge0.1$ in MAGMA's gene--gene correlation, against $\nLipNeighbours$ in humans.

\emph{A permutation null.} Real data cannot test calibration, because no pathway is known to be null. We therefore permuted the dry-biomass phenotype $100$ times and ran each permutation through the full GWAS, MAGMA and CATFISH pipeline ($304$ pathways, $30{,}400$ null tests; Section~2.7). Without gene-level genomic control, the per-pathway $p$-values were not uniform: $7.2\%$ of null pathways had $p<0.05$ and $2.3\%$ $p<0.01$ although $\lambda$ was $0.84$, the excess grew with pathway size from $6.1\%$ to $12.7\%$, and $18$ of $100$ permutations had a family-wise false positive (family-wise error $0.18$, $95\%$ CI $0.11$--$0.27$; Figure~\ref{fig:sorghum_dry}a,b,d; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:sorghum_null}). The cause was a genome-wide shift: each permutation moved all gene $z$-scores together (SD of the permutation mean $0.24$, against $0.075$ expected under the correlated null), genes on different chromosomes still correlated $0.06$ across permutations, and the number of pathways with $p<0.05$ tracked the mean gene $z$-score (Spearman $0.90$; Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:supp_sorghum_null}a). A self-contained test reads such a shift as signal that grows with pathway size. Gene-level genomic control removed it: $\lambda$ became $1.01$, $5.2\%$ of null pathways had $p<0.05$ and $1.3\%$ $p<0.01$, with no dependence on size ($5.0$--$6.0\%$), and the family-wise error fell to $0.12$ ($12/98$; $95\%$ CI $0.06$--$0.20$), with all $21$ family-wise false positives in different pathways.

The remaining excess lies in the extreme tail. After centring, per-pathway $p$-values below $0.001$ were $1.8\times$ as frequent as expected, and because the family minimum is a tail statistic, small per-pathway excesses grow at the family level. MAGMA overstated moderate gene--gene correlations (pairs reported at $r=0.52$ correlated $0.32$ across permutations; Figure~\ref{fig:sorghum_dry}c), but replacing them with the correlations observed across permutations did not lower the family-wise error ($0.15$, $95\%$ CI $0.09$--$0.24$). The source is upstream: variant $p$-values below $10^{-4}$ were $4.5\times$ too frequent overall and $11.5\times$ at minor allele frequency $0.01$--$0.02$ (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:supp_sorghum_null}b), so the mixed-model test is heavy-tailed here while the multivariate normal null is Gaussian. We did not filter variants to correct this, since the filter would be tuned to the outcome. In this panel, CATFISH with genomic control gives calibrated per-pathway $p$-values, and its family-wise $p$-values are about $2.5\times$ anti-conservative ($0.12$ against $0.05$), from the tail of the variant-level test.

\emph{Dry biomass.} In the real data no pathway reached family-wise significance; the leading pathway was proanthocyanidin biosynthesis from flavanols (family-wise $p=\nSorTopFwerDry$; Figure~\ref{fig:sorghum_dry}e). The top gene, Sobic.004G014400, encodes cycloartenol synthase, the first committed step of sterol biosynthesis, and is one of two top-ten genes supported by all three layers (Figure~\ref{fig:sorghum_dry}f): it shares its lead variant with seven neighbours but leads them on MAGMA evidence, and the pathway layer moves it from rank $\nSorCasRankNp$ to first. Cycloartenol synthase loss is lethal in \textit{Arabidopsis} and sterol-deficient mutants are impaired in cellulose synthesis \citep{Babiychuk2008,Schrick2012}, and in bioenergy sorghum the gene is highest in young elongating internodes \citep{Kebrom2017}; a link to biomass remains untested. The fourth-ranked gene, Sobic.008G029200, has a WRKY DNA-binding domain but draws its pathway support from a SorghumBicolorCyc entry that assigns it acyl-ACP thioesterase activity, so here the pathway layer amplified an annotation error. The eight pre-specified flowering-time and height genes are in no tested pathway, so the pathway layer could not move them.

\subsection*{3.7 The gene score resolves a new sorghum sugar--fibre locus to two enzymes}

Dry biomass showed the procedure was calibrated in this panel but could not show the gene score adding findings. We therefore analysed three stem-composition traits in the same lines (Section~2.7): Brix at maturity (soluble solids in stem juice), neutral detergent fibre (NDF; cell wall) and non-fibre carbohydrate (NFC; mainly sugars and starch). Brix correlated with NDF ($r=\nBapRBrixNDF$) and NFC ($r=\nBapRBrixNFC$): lines with more stem sugar have less cell wall, the trade-off that separates sweet from biomass sorghum. With family-wise control, the self-contained test called $\nBapSigBrix$, $\nBapSigNDF$ and $\nBapSigNFC$ of $\nBapNpw$ pathways, and the competitive test $\nBapCompBrix$, $\nBapCompNDF$ and $\nBapCompNFC$. The significant pathways, \nBapSigListBrix (Brix), \nBapSigListNDF (NDF) and \nBapSigListNFC (NFC), include two single-gene pathways whose gene lies in the chromosome~4 locus described next, so, as in the simulation (Section~3.2), the self-contained test flags them and the competitive test does not (family-wise $p=\nBapBalaCompBrix$, $\nBapTrxPwCompNDF$, $\nBapTrxPwCompNFC$). These calls restate the locus through its annotations rather than implicate $\beta$-alanine or thioredoxin metabolism.

\emph{One locus, two enzymes.} The gene scores mapped the three traits to one locus. Of $\nBapNgenes$ genes, $\nBapNshared$ were in the top 100 for all three traits, all within $85\,\mathrm{kb}$ on chromosome~4 ($63.96$--$64.04\,\mathrm{Mb}$; Figure~\ref{fig:bap_chr4}d; Table~\ref{tab:bap_chr4}), a region not previously reported for sugar or fibre. The two genes at the locus with pathway evidence lead the rankings. Sobic.004G300800, the ALDH3 fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase \textit{SbALDH3E1}, predicted to be chloroplast-localised \citep{Islam2022SbALDH}, ranked $\nBapAldhBrix$ (Brix), $\nBapAldhNDF$ (NDF) and $\nBapAldhNFC$ (NFC); the neighbouring Sobic.004G300900, an NADPH-dependent thioredoxin reductase (TRXR), ranked $\nBapTrxBrix$, $\nBapTrxNDF$ and $\nBapTrxNFC$. Among genes with evidence in all three layers, \textit{SbALDH3E1} was first for Brix and TRXR second for both fibre traits. Neither gene holds the top variants: for Brix their best variant is shared with four neighbours, and for NDF and NFC the strongest variants lie about $60\,\mathrm{kb}$ away in the unannotated Sobic.004G301200--301700. The two enzymes lead on the other layers, with the two smallest MAGMA $p$-values in the locus for all three traits, and they are the only genes of the locus in any pathway. GWAS alone would place the fibre signal on the unannotated genes; the gene score moves it to the adjacent enzymes.

Part of their pathway evidence is their own, since the two significant pathways that contain them owe their significance largely to them. When each pathway was rescored without the gene on the same null draws (Figure~\ref{fig:bap_chr4}e), \textit{SbALDH3E1} kept its place for Brix: rank $\nBapGmAldhBrix$ without the pathway layer, $\nBapAldhBrix$ with it and $\nBapLogoAldhBrix$ without its own contribution. TRXR kept most of its lift for the fibre traits, ranking $\nBapLogoTrxNDF$ (NDF) and $\nBapLogoTrxNFC$ (NFC) without its own contribution, against $\nBapTrxNDF$ and $\nBapTrxNFC$ with it and $\nBapGmTrxNDF$ and $\nBapGmTrxNFC$ without pathways. For the other trait of each gene, the lift was its own: TRXR fell back to rank $\nBapLogoTrxBrix$ for Brix, and \textit{SbALDH3E1} to $\nBapLogoAldhNDF$ and $\nBapLogoAldhNFC$ for the fibre traits. Across all pathway-supported genes in each trait's top 100, $\nBapLogoKeepBrix$ of $\nBapLogoNBrix$ (Brix), $\nBapLogoKeepNDF$ of $\nBapLogoNNDF$ (NDF) and $\nBapLogoKeepNFC$ of $\nBapLogoNNFC$ (NFC) stayed in the top 100 without their own contribution, and their median ranks lay between those with and without the pathway layer: in this panel, about half of the lift comes from the other genes of a gene's pathways.

\emph{One haplotype, a composition effect.} The lead variants of the three traits are in strong LD: the Brix lead at $\nBapLeadPosBrix\,\mathrm{Mb}$, $6\,\mathrm{kb}$ from the dehydrogenase, and the shared NDF/NFC lead at $\nBapLeadPosNDF\,\mathrm{Mb}$ have $r^2=\nBapLeadRsq$, and across $\nBapNwin$ variants the Brix and NDF $z$-scores align (correlation $\nBapZcor$; Figure~\ref{fig:bap_chr4}c). The signal survived three principal components. Per copy, the Brix-raising allele (\nBapUp, frequency $\nBapFreqUp$) increased Brix by $\nBapEffBrix$ and NFC by $\nBapEffNFC$ standard deviations and decreased NDF by $|\nBapEffNDF|$ (Figure~\ref{fig:bap_chr4}b). With Brix in the model, NDF still differed by $\nBapAdjNDF$ points between homozygotes ($p=\nBapAdjP$), so the sugar--fibre trade-off explains about $\nBapFracVia\%$ of the allele's fibre effect (Figure~\ref{fig:bap_chr4}a). The locus is not a maturity or height locus: no cloned maturity or height gene lies on chromosome~4, the strongest flowering-time association in these lines had $p=1.1\times10^{-4}$, and the Brix-raising allele had no detectable effect on stalk height ($p=0.29$) or dry biomass ($p=0.09$). In public RNA-seq data, \textit{SbALDH3E1} is expressed in leaf, shoot, root, stem and inflorescence, highest in the leaf sheath at flowering and lowest in stem internodes (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:sbaldh_expression}) \citep{Moreno2022ExpressionAtlas}. A maize fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase is a candidate for kernel oil content \citep{Zhang2026FALDH}, and \textit{Arabidopsis} ALDH3F1 supplies acetate for histone acetylation at \textit{FLOWERING LOCUS C} \citep{Xu2020ALDH3F1}.

%% ---- algorithm ----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{CATFISH self-contained test: per-pathway and family-wise $p$-values from one set of correlated null draws (Section~2.3).}
\label{alg:catfish}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require gene $p$-values $p_g$ from MAGMA for $G$ genes; MAGMA gene--gene correlations $r_{ij}$; pathways $\mathcal{F}=\{S_1,\dots,S_K\}$; number of null draws $B$; omnibus $\mathcal{O}$, the ACAT combination of ACAT, Fisher, Stouffer, minP and soft TFisher (truncation grid fixed in advance)
\Ensure per-pathway $p$-value $\tilde p_k$ and family-wise $p$-value $\tilde p^{\mathrm{WY}}_k$ for every pathway
\State $z_g \gets \Phi^{-1}(1-p_g)$ computed from $\log p_g$; $\;p_g \gets 1-\Phi\{z_g-\mathrm{median}(z)\}$ \Comment{gene-level genomic control}
\For{each chromosome $c$}
  \State $R_c \gets I + \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{P}_c} r_{ij}\,(e_ie_j^{\top}+e_je_i^{\top})$; floor eigenvalues at $10^{-6}$ if not positive definite
  \State $R_c = U_c^{\top}U_c$ \Comment{Cholesky factor}
\EndFor
\For{$k=1,\dots,K$}
  \State $q_k \gets \mathcal{O}\{\mathbf{p}(S_k)\}$ \Comment{observed omnibus, in log space}
\EndFor
\For{$b=1,\dots,B$}
  \State $\mathbf{Z}^{(b)}_c \gets U_c^{\top}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_c$, $\;\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_c\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$, independently for each chromosome \Comment{one genome-wide draw}\label{alg:draw}
  \State $p^{(b)}_g \gets 1-\Phi(Z^{(b)}_g)$ for every gene\label{alg:nullp}
  \For{$k=1,\dots,K$}
    \State $Q^{(b)}_k \gets \mathcal{O}\{\mathbf{p}^{(b)}(S_k)\}$ \Comment{every pathway scored on the same draw}
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\For{$k=1,\dots,K$}
  \State $\tilde p_k \gets \bigl\{1+\sum_{b}\mathbf{1}(Q^{(b)}_k\le q_k)\bigr\}/(B+1)$ \Comment{per-pathway $p$-value}
  \State $\mu_k,\sigma_k \gets$ mean and SD of $Q^{(1)}_k,\dots,Q^{(B)}_k$; $\;z_k \gets (q_k-\mu_k)/\sigma_k$; $\;\zeta^{(b)}_k \gets (Q^{(b)}_k-\mu_k)/\sigma_k$
\EndFor
\For{$b=1,\dots,B$}
  \State $M^{(b)} \gets \min_k \zeta^{(b)}_k$ \Comment{family minimum of one draw}
\EndFor
\For{$k=1,\dots,K$}
  \State $\tilde p^{\mathrm{WY}}_k \gets \bigl\{1+\sum_{b}\mathbf{1}(M^{(b)}\le z_k)\bigr\}/(B+1)$ \Comment{Westfall--Young; ties count as extreme}
\EndFor
\State \Return $\tilde p_k$, $\tilde p^{\mathrm{WY}}_k$ and $z_k$ (the pathway statistic $z_{\mathrm{std}}$ used by the gene score)
\end{algorithmic}
\vspace{2pt}
\footnotesize Competitive test: lines~\ref{alg:draw}--\ref{alg:nullp} are replaced by one circular shift $s$ of the observed gene $p$-values along the genome order, shared by all pathways ($p^{(b)}_g \gets p_{g+s}$); everything else is unchanged. A pathway is called by both tests when $\max(\tilde p^{\mathrm{WY,self}}_k,\tilde p^{\mathrm{WY,comp}}_k)\le\alpha$.
\end{algorithm}

\section{4 Discussion}

\subsection*{4.1 What the Westfall--Young layer buys, and what it does not}

The Westfall--Young layer’s contribution is limited and should be described as such. Reading the null matrix across rows instead of down columns is free—the draws are already taken—and makes the family-wise statement exact for the pathway database’s true overlap structure, without estimating or describing it. It does not change any pathway’s marginal evidence (the column-wise result) or improve power. In shared-universe simulations with no within-pathway correlation, where the analytic $p$-value is valid, the Westfall--Young threshold was $\nWyGainLo$ to $\nWyGainHi$ times the Bonferroni threshold $\nBonfThrK$: slightly more permissive due to accounting for overlap averaging $\nBrkMembership$ pathways per gene. This can affect borderline results but will not fix deeper problems in a flawed analysis.

The ratio of the Bonferroni threshold to the Westfall--Young threshold is diagnostically useful: it summarizes how close a pathway family is to independent. A ratio near 1 suggests near-independence and that Bonferroni is adequate; a ratio far from 1 indicates Bonferroni is miscalibrated for that gene-set collection. The ratio is easy to compute as a by-product of the procedure but is rarely reported.

However, this interpretation requires calibrated per-test statistics. In simulations with within-pathway correlation, the threshold on the analytic scale was $\nWyStrictLo$ to $\nWyStrictHi$ times stricter than Bonferroni, not due to stronger dependence but because analytic $p$-values were anti-conservative and Westfall--Young corrected this using the same null. On that scale, the ratio conflates per-test miscalibration with test dependence and cannot disentangle them.

We were wrong about one aspect and correct it here. We expected the size-standardized Westfall--Young variant of Section~2.3 to remove the dependence of pathway evidence on pathway size, because comparing each statistic to its own null bank should put pathways on a common scale. It did not: in the maize data the size--evidence correlation shifted only from $-0.807$ to $-0.800$ in the human analysis and was essentially unchanged elsewhere. The per-size null banks have standard deviations that differ by only a few percent across the pathway sizes we examined, so standardization divides by an almost constant factor and cannot remove a dependence arising in the numerator. The size dependence is in the observed statistics; in a saturated regime it reflects real signal accumulation, not a scaling artefact. Standardization is still worth reporting because it can change individual rankings, but it does not correct size bias and should not be presented as doing so.

\subsection*{4.2 Saturation and the self-contained hypothesis}

We believe the LDL-cholesterol result in Section~3.4 is this paper’s most consequential finding, and it is not about CATFISH. Self-contained gene-set tests ask whether genes in a set show more association than the null of no association \citep{Goeman2007,Maciejewski2014}. With $n\approx1.3$ million for a highly polygenic trait, the answer is yes for almost any gene set: $11.2\%$ of all genes are individually genome-wide significant, and a random set of fifty genes rejects the self-contained null with probability $0.997$. Nothing has malfunctioned; the hypothesis no longer discriminates.

Three consequences follow. First, as GWAS sample sizes grow, self-contained tests will increasingly fail silently, yielding long lists of highly significant pathways that appear meaningful. Second, multiple-testing refinements do not fix this: even with strong family-wise control, $\nLipSigLDL$ of $\nLipNpw$ pathways remained significant for LDL-C ($\nLipSigHDL$ for HDL-C). A stricter threshold in a saturated analysis is simply a more carefully wrong answer. Third, the competitive hypothesis—whether genes in a set are \emph{more} associated than genes outside it—does not saturate the same way because it is benchmarked against the observed genome-wide background rather than no association. MAGMA’s competitive test on the same data supports this: in Europeans it found $5$ (LDL-C) and $12$ (HDL-C) significant pathways out of $1{,}390$, versus $1{,}358$ and $1{,}383$ under MAGMA’s self-contained test, and ranked plasma lipoprotein assembly, remodeling and clearance first for both traits (Section~3.4). Thus, for large, well-powered human GWAS, the competitive framing is appropriate. CATFISH’s self-contained framing is more natural when few genes are associated and the genome-wide background is near-null, as in sorghum, where it found no significant pathways and per-pathway $p$-values were near nominal (Section~3.6). We do not claim greater power than competitive tests in that regime; we show ranking and calibration, not a power advantage.

This bounds our method’s applicability, and we prefer to state it now rather than learn it in review. It also guides how to read the human results. In this regime, significance is uninformative, but ranking is: the top of both lists mixes lipoprotein pathways with large generic sets that accrue evidence simply because, in a saturated genome, any large set is enriched. When pathways are compared with others of similar size, lipoprotein pathways are extreme in both traits (Section~3.4). Thus, in a saturated analysis, a self-contained method should output a size-aware ranking and a gene list, not a list of significant sets.

\subsection*{4.3 What the ranking and the gene score add when significance
saturates}

Lipid analyses show that saturation eliminates the significance list but preserves pathway ordering, which requires two inputs to interpret. First is pathway size: in a saturated genome, large sets accrue evidence and dominate the top ranks, while a relevant small set can be extreme for its size yet still rank in the hundreds. Comparing pathways only to others of similar size recovered the plasma-lipoprotein subtree for both traits (Figure~\ref{fig:lipid_size}). This size-matched comparison is descriptive; an inferential version would require a null distribution of size-matched ranks, potentially via the Section~2.3 resampling, which we have not implemented.

Pathway results are most useful when mapped to genes. If genes are ranked by their strongest variant, they capture locus-wide evidence; if ranked by gene-level tests, strong signals from a few variants can be missed. A combined score recovered established genes for both traits better than either layer alone across the tested weights, because support from multiple layers lifts genes while single-layer support drops them. However, the improvement is modest and partly circular: pathway membership favours well-studied genes, so performance on established genes likely overstates performance for genes absent from pathway databases. The score ranks loci first, because established genes often have LD neighbours with near-identical association evidence. Within a locus, however, it does more: where the GWAS cannot separate genes, the score puts the gene with the relevant function ahead of its LD neighbours. \textit{APOE} ranked above \textit{TOMM40}, with which it shares its lead variant, in nine of the ten lipid sets (Table~\ref{tab:apoe_locus}), and in sorghum the gene score resolved a locus of some fifteen genes to a fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase and a thioredoxin reductase (Section~3.7). It cannot separate genes that are tied on all three layers, such as \textit{APOE} and \textit{APOC1}, and only fine-mapping or functional data can show which gene is causal.

Across nine traits, pathway evidence improved the candidate list as a whole, not only at the lipid loci. Against the same score without its pathway layer, it placed $\nBenchGainTwenty\%$ more gold-standard genes in the genome-wide top $20$, and the gain held in every trait (Section~3.5). With it, CATFISH matched PoPS, a method trained on tens of thousands of gene features, leading where pathway curation is strong and trailing in immune traits, and the two rankings were complementary. This argues for treating curated pathways as a source of prior information on genes, not only as a unit of inference.

\subsection*{4.4 Two regimes, two kinds of validation}

The lipid and sorghum analyses validate different aspects of the method. Lipid traits have strong signal and short-range LD; meaningful ordering is supported by convergence, as two traits with largely disjoint gene-level signals implicate the same pathway via different branches. They say little about false positives because the null is rarely true. The sorghum trait has sparse signal and much denser LD; validation comes from calibration: the independence-based omnibus over-rejected, calibrated per-pathway $p$-values were near nominal, family-wise control found nothing, and permutations directly estimated error rates (Section~3.6). A method claiming pathway discoveries here would be suspect; this one only ranked pathways and genes without unsupported significance claims.

Two lessons generalize. First, validate the gene-level model before interpreting pathway results: in the BAP, MAGMA’s multi-model output was inflated by gene size, and even an LD-aware correlated pathway null would have propagated that inflation as pathway signal. Second, curated annotation can help or mislead: it pushed cycloartenol synthase to the top, but also elevated a WRKY transcription factor via an inappropriate enzymatic annotation. For candidates whose rank relies on the pathway layer, verify the underlying annotation; the per-layer scoring makes this easy.

\subsection*{4.5 Calibration on real genotypes}

The simulations and permutation null address different questions. Simulations sample gene $z$-scores from the null correlation model and yielded nominal per-pathway and family-wise error rates across all LD structures tested (Sections~3.1--3.3). The permutation null runs real genotypes through the full pipeline (Section~3.6) and revealed two departures from the model, neither due to LD modeling.

First, GWAS from the same panel can differ by a genome-wide shift in gene $z$-scores. Because this shift is shared across chromosomes, no gene--gene correlation matrix captures it. It is one value per GWAS, so median-centring the gene $z$-scores removes it. Centring restores nominal per-pathway calibration (inflation factor $1.01$, type I error $0.052$) and eliminates the dependence of false positives on pathway size. It does not change the data or gene ranking and is essentially free, so we use it in all real analyses and recommend it by default. The drawback is in saturated GWAS, where the median gene is truly associated and centring removes real polygenic signal; in that setting, it shifts the self-contained question toward the competitive one, which Section~4.2 argues is preferable.

The second issue is the tail of the variant-level test. After centring, the family-wise error rate was $0.12$, driven by variant $p$-values more extreme than expected, especially at minor allele frequencies below $0.05$. In a densely linked panel, a plausible cause is mis-specified MAGMA gene correlations. Indeed, correlations from raw genotypes overstate moderate correlation after mixed-model adjustment for population structure. However, replacing these correlations did not reduce the family-wise error, and overstated correlation is conservative. The problem lies in the input, not the correlation model, and a Gaussian-by-construction null cannot absorb it. Removing rare variants until the null fit would tune the analysis, so we report the excess. For panels like the BAP, the per-pathway calibrated $p$-value is the reliable output; a family-wise $p$-value near $0.05$ is weaker evidence than its nominal level.

Neither departure changes the conclusions of the main analyses, which use centred gene $p$-values. In sorghum, no pathway was family-wise significant. Centring reduced calibrated pathways with $p<0.05$ from $14.5\%$ to $7.2\%$, near the permutation-null rate; a false-positive–prone test still found none. In the lipid data, rankings were robust: the plasma lipoprotein pathway ranked first in all ten trait–ancestry sets with centring and in nine without (European HDL-C, third). Spearman correlations of pathway statistics with vs. without centring were $0.96$--$0.99$, and family-wise significant pathways dropped by $11$--$24\%$. The largest losses were among large generic pathways: in Europeans, the median size of the top 20 pathways fell from $159$ to $118$ genes (LDL-C) and from $172$ to $120$ (HDL-C).

\subsection*{4.6 Numerical precision is a correctness issue}

Section~2.4 is not an implementation note, but it reads like one. Without moving to log space, the top-pathway list would have depended on file order, with leading $p$-values stuck at the double-precision floor and no indication of error. We saw this failure twice during development—once from ACAT overflow and once from combined-$p$ underflow—and both times it produced a plausible-looking table.

Simulations revealed a third issue in a different component. The soft TFisher code returns the null CDF of its statistic; the $p$-value is one minus that, but beyond $1-10^{-16}$ the difference rounds to zero. In the exchangeable design with $\rho=0.6$ and $m=50$, about $3.5\%$ of null pathways hit this limit and were reported at the common lower floor. With a $10^{-50}$ floor, the empirical family-wise error rate was $0.24$. Lowering the floor to $10^{-300}$ to “restore” resolution increased it to $1.00$: the artificial value was smaller than any other component’s output, so the omnibus family minimum became the same shared number in almost every family and the Westfall--Young threshold landed on the tie. The fix is to cap this component at its true precision. With a $10^{-16}$ cap, other components with closed-form tails (which still resolve distinct values below $10^{-100}$) determine the family minimum, restoring a continuous statistic. A floor asserts resolution; setting it below what the computation can achieve creates manufactured ties that mimic extreme evidence.

We suggest that pathway methods for modern large-$n$ GWAS summary statistics explicitly state how they handle $p$-values below $10^{-300}$, and that reviewers verify this. The arithmetic is simple, but failures are invisible unless checked. A basic diagnostic is to count pathways sharing the smallest reported $p$-value; if more than one or two do, rankings below that value are not meaningful. For family-wise procedures, check how many distinct values the null family minimum takes; a threshold from a distribution with a large atom is not a valid threshold.

\subsection*{4.7 Practical recommendations}

Recommendations: Before any pathway analysis, inspect the gene-level null. A median gene-level $p$-value far from $0.5$ suggests either true saturation or population-structure inflation, which call for different remedies (competitive testing vs a mixed model). Also check that the null does not drift with gene size; under strong LD, MAGMA top-variant and multi-model $p$-values can inflate with the number of variants (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:sorghum_pcol}). Centre gene $z$-scores at their median before testing (Section~2.7) to remove per-GWAS shifts that otherwise inflate self-contained tests with pathway size. If genotypes are available, use a phenotype-permutation null to assess whether family-wise $p$-values are trustworthy (Section~3.6). When gene--gene correlations are available, calibrate to the correlated null and confirm the correlation file covers all chromosomes; we once parsed only chromosome~1, silently treating $85\%$ of the genome as independent. Report per-pathway calibrated and Westfall--Young family-wise $p$-values, plus the Bonferroni threshold and its ratio to the Westfall--Young threshold on calibrated statistics (Section~4.1), as the comparison is informative. Before trusting any family-wise threshold, count the number of distinct values in the null family minimum. With adaptive soft TFisher, use a truncation grid spanning small and large thresholds; a grid restricted to small $\tau$ leaves most small pathways with an empty statistic. Treat the ranking, not the significance list, as the main output, and validate against independent biology (as in our maize, lipid and sorghum analyses). In saturated settings, compare pathways to others of similar size, interpret candidate-gene lists as locus rankings, and check the annotation for any candidate whose rank depends on the pathway layer.

\subsection*{4.8 From a shared locus to candidate genes for validation}

Stem-composition analyses clarify the gene score’s purpose. A GWAS of three correlated traits identified a ~$100\,\mathrm{kb}$ haplotype on chromosome~4 in strong LD ($r^2\ge0.8$ between lead variants), containing ~15 genes with largely interchangeable variant evidence. GWAS alone cannot prioritise a causal gene, and for the two fibre traits the top variants implicate unannotated genes. The gene score instead asks which locus gene has the strongest gene-level signal and a known metabolic role. Across all three traits, two genes stand out: the fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase \textit{SbALDH3E1} and an NADPH-dependent thioredoxin reductase. Both rank in the top $35$ genome-wide for each trait, and when evidence from all three layers is required, one is ranked first or second per trait. For editing, this reduces the search from ~$34{,}000$ genes to two candidates that most clearly link stem sugar and fibre in this panel.

The locus suggests a shift in stem carbon allocation between soluble and structural pools. The Brix-raising haplotype increased sugar and non-fibre carbohydrate and decreased fibre by similar amounts (standard units), with at most weak effects on flowering and harvest date and none on height or biomass. Thus, lines carrying it are not sweeter because they are later, taller, or smaller, and about half of the fibre reduction is not explained by higher sugar. This points to a gene affecting cell-wall deposition or upstream carbon partitioning rather than sucrose accumulation alone. Both candidates could plausibly act via different routes. ALDH3 enzymes oxidise medium- and long-chain aldehydes to acids, linking lipid turnover and aldehyde detoxification to fatty-acid and acetate pools \citep{Islam2022SbALDH}; in \textit{Arabidopsis}, ALDH3F1 contributes to the acetyl-CoA pool used for histone acetylation and regulation of a developmental gene \citep{Xu2020ALDH3F1}, and in maize a FALDH gene is a candidate for kernel oil content \citep{Zhang2026FALDH}. Thioredoxin reductases maintain reduced thioredoxins, which regulate many plastid carbon-metabolism enzymes via reversible disulphide reduction. We found no reports linking either gene to stem sugar or cell walls in sorghum or other species, and we do not claim either is causal.

Four points qualify the result. First, the pathway evidence elevating the two genes is mostly self-driven: the only significant pathways containing them ($\beta$-alanine biosynthesis~I and the thioredoxin pathway) owe their significance to these genes and are not significant in the competitive test. Thus the pathway layer functions as an annotation prior, ranking genes with known enzymatic function above unannotated neighbours; this is useful but biased if the causal gene is unannotated (Section~4.4). Leaving each gene out of its own pathways measures how much of this is circular (Section~3.7): without its own contribution \textit{SbALDH3E1} still ranked $\nBapLogoAldhBrix$ for Brix, and the thioredoxin reductase $\nBapLogoTrxNDF$ and $\nBapLogoTrxNFC$ for the fibre traits, so their lead over the unannotated genes of the locus does not rest only on their own association; their lifts for the other traits did. Second, the locus is a single haplotype carried by $\nBapNUpHom$ of $\nBapNlines$ lines (including Rio). Adding three principal components barely changed the association, but a haplotype restricted to related lines may still reflect group history; biparental populations or near-isogenic lines would address this. Third, \textit{SbALDH3E1} is weakly expressed in stem internodes and highest in leaves and leaf sheaths, so if causal it may act via leaf/sheath dry matter (used for NDF/NFC) or carbon supply to the stem rather than the sampled internode. Fourth, the descriptive regression separating fibre from sugar ignores relatedness, and effect sizes for a significance-selected lead variant are upwardly biased in a panel of this size.

Next steps, given these limits, are to assess allele/haplotype-specific expression in stem, sheath and leaf; compare coding and regulatory differences between haplotypes; and create loss-of-function alleles of \textit{SbALDH3E1} and thioredoxin reductase via mutagenesis or genome editing, then score Brix and fibre. The method narrows the locus to two genes and reports, for each, how much its rank is driven by GWAS, gene tests and pathway annotation.

\section{5 Limitations and future work}

Several limitations constrain our conclusions.

\textbf{No simulated GWAS.} The simulations in Section~2.6 model only the omnibus statistic and its null, using block-structured and exchangeable gene--gene correlation. We did not simulate genotypes, assign causal variants, generate phenotypes, or run the full GWAS--MAGMA--CATFISH pipeline. This natural next step would assess power under realistic genetic architectures rather than latent mean shifts and was intentionally omitted. Therefore, the power curves in Section~3.1 reflect stylised gene-level $z$-score signals, not realistic genetic architectures.

\textbf{Unreported correlations are set to zero.} The correlation matrices in Section~2.3 are formed by inserting MAGMA-reported gene pairs into an identity matrix, so any unreported pair is treated as independent. This is usually reasonable for distant genes and yields a banded matrix, but nearby unreported pairs (e.g., crossing batch boundaries or involving a dropped gene) understate dependence and can be anti-conservative. Section~3.3 quantifies this effect and shows it is not shared equally: the family-wise reading degrades faster than the marginal one because the family minimum is a tail statistic, where correlation misspecification is most damaging. Chromosome-coverage checks catch gross failures; \texttt{10\_pair\_coverage.R} measures finer gaps, which we recommend reporting alongside any family-wise $p$-value.

\textbf{Simplified LD models.} True gene--gene correlation decays with distance. The shared-universe simulations approximate LD with independent exchangeable blocks, so pathways spanning blocks are mostly uncorrelated; the exchangeable design correlates all pathway members, exaggerating LD and serving only as a stress test. Neither captures gradual distance-based decay. Real-data analyses use empirical MAGMA correlations and avoid this limitation.

\textbf{What strong control rests on.} The strong family-wise control in Section~2.3 is exact only under its model. It assumes association shifts mean gene $z$-scores while leaving correlations among unassociated genes unchanged, and it defines a null pathway as one with no associated genes. Under linkage disequilibrium, a gene may appear associated only because it tags a causal variant in a nearby gene; a pathway containing such a gene is non-null under the self-contained gene-level hypothesis even if it contains no causal gene. Rejecting it is therefore not a family-wise error, though it may be a biological false positive. Strong control also depends on an adequate correlation matrix; Section~3.3 shows family-wise calibration degrades quickly when the matrix is incomplete. We did not separately verify strong control by simulation; under the model it follows from the complete-null results.

\textbf{Parametric null.} Null draws use a multivariate normal correlation model rather than phenotype permutations. Although Westfall--Young in form, validity depends on the correlation model (Section~3.3). The sorghum permutation null (Section~3.6) directly checks this: after genomic control, per-pathway calibration was nominal but the family-wise error rate was $0.12$, likely because variant-level tests have heavier tails than a Gaussian null. We tested one panel and one trait; results may differ with weaker LD, larger samples, or better-calibrated association tests. We recommend a permutation null before relying on family-wise $p$-values in a new panel.

\textbf{Soft TFisher resolution.} Soft TFisher cannot resolve $p$-values below $\sim 10^{-16}$, causing ties among highly enriched pathways. Other components resolve smaller $p$-values and can dominate the omnibus ranking; implementing the soft-threshold tail on the log scale would remove this limit.

\textbf{Partial comparison with other methods.} We compared CATFISH with MAGMA gene-set analysis on lipid data (Section~3.4) and in simulations with known truth (Section~3.2) assessing calibration and power. Simulations used one LD panel, ten chromosomes, and five signal architectures, and did not include other gene-set methods.

\textbf{Incomplete cross-species replication.} Westfall--Young was run on maize, sorghum, and human data. The sorghum stem-volume dataset from the original CATFISH paper could not be re-analysed under the new calibration because the MAGMA \texttt{.genes.raw} file was missing; Table~\ref{tab:density} therefore reports signal density from gene-level output only. The Section~3.6 sorghum analysis uses the BAP panel and, with $330$ lines, is underpowered for pathway discovery, focusing on calibration and gene ranking rather than new pathway detection.

\textbf{Tail resolution.} Monte Carlo calibration resolves $p$-values to $1/(B+1)$. CATFISH can extrapolate extreme tails via a generalized Pareto fit \citep{Knijnenburg2009}, but needs enough valid null draws; a single missing draw can disable it. In one human run, $638$ of $1{,}348$ pathways tied at the Monte Carlo floor. We now require only half (not all) permutations, but have not validated this at production scale.

\textbf{Dependence on upstream choices.} Results depend on the SNP-to-gene window, LD reference panel, gene model, and pathway database, as with similar methods. The $10\,$kb windows used here are standard but may not be trait-optimal.

\section{6 Conclusion}

Pathway analysis of GWAS summary statistics has two dependence problems, but most work addresses only the easier one. Gene–gene correlation within pathways is routinely modeled; pathway–pathway correlation from shared gene membership is often “corrected” by assuming it away. We add a Westfall–Young max-statistic layer to CATFISH that provides strong family-wise error control under this dependence, uses the same gene–gene correlation model already needed for calibration (no extra estimation), and quantifies how far Bonferroni deviates from the truth for a given database when applied to calibrated statistics.

Two broader results follow. First, computing the omnibus in log space is required at modern GWAS sample sizes; otherwise a silent failure can occur. Second, self-contained pathway testing saturates: in a GWAS of $1.3$ million individuals, $11.2\%$ of genes are genome-wide significant, a random 50-gene set rejects with probability $0.997$, yet strong FWER control leaves $\nLipSigLDL$ of $\nLipNpw$ pathways significant. This reflects the hypothesis, not the correction, and ranking remains informative: plasma lipoprotein assembly/remodeling/clearance leads the LDL-C ordering, lipoprotein pathways are extreme for size in both LDL-C and HDL-C, and gene prioritization recovers known lipid genes better than GWAS association alone. In a sorghum panel with much denser LD and low power, the same procedure finds no significant pathways—as expected for a calibrated method—and a genotype-permutation null shows that centering gene $z$-scores calibrates each pathway while FWER remains above nominal. When few genes are associated, CATFISH’s self-contained question is natural; whether it outperforms competitive tests for weak signals remains open.

\subsection*{Supplementary Note S1: The five pathway signal archetypes}

This note extends Section~3.1. A pathway’s \emph{ranked profile} is its gene-level $-\log_{10}p$ values sorted from largest to smallest; an archetype is the profile’s shape. Archetypes are statistical templates: they are not inferred from real pathways, real pathways may mix features of several, and this paper does not assign real pathways to archetypes.

\subsubsection*{S1.1 Strong Sparse Signal (SSS)}

In an SSS pathway, most association evidence comes from a small subset of genes, while the rest are near null. The ranked profile has one or a few very small ordered $p$-values, then quickly drops to null-like values. Thus the pathway signal is driven by a few high-evidence genes rather than broad enrichment, so head-focused statistics such as ACAT and minP should be most efficient. Methods that weight all genes equally dilute these leading genes with the null majority.

SSS is expected when pathway behaviour is controlled by one or a few bottleneck genes (e.g., a committed-step enzyme, rate-limiting regulator, or essential transporter) \citep{MorenoSanchez2008,Naz2023,Tanner2018,Song2020}. For example, aspartokinase initiates the aspartate-derived amino-acid pathway that produces lysine, threonine, methionine and isoleucine \citep{Galili2011} by phosphorylating aspartate to aspartyl phosphate \citep{Hasenoehrl2019}. Variation in this entry enzyme can create a strong localised signal while downstream genes remain buffered or near null. In the maize grain-nitrogen analysis (Section~2.7), the top candidate gene is an aspartate kinase isozyme, and the top pathway, L-homoserine biosynthesis, is the branch whose first step it catalyses.

\subsubsection*{S1.2 Single-Gene Proxy (SGP)}

In an SGP pathway, significance is driven by one strongly associated gene while the rest are near null; the ranked profile shows a single extreme peak followed by an otherwise null-like distribution. Statistically, SGP is a boundary case of SSS, with minP and ACAT again the most sensitive components. Interpretively, however, the pathway adds little beyond the dominant gene-level signal.

Biologically, SGP occurs when a pathway is effectively defined by one major gene, often in small or overlapping annotations. Phenylalanine metabolism illustrates this. \textit{PAH} encodes phenylalanine hydroxylase, the rate-limiting enzyme converting phenylalanine to tyrosine, and pathogenic \textit{PAH} variation is the main cause of phenylketonuria and most PAH deficiency \citep{Flydal2013PAH,Elhawary2022PKU}. Broader phenylalanine-metabolism pathways include genes for tetrahydrobiopterin synthesis, recycling, chaperoning, and related processes, but the dominance of \textit{PAH} means pathway significance may reflect a gene-centric signal rather than coordinated enrichment. Accordingly, pathways significant only by minP and ACAT should be interpreted cautiously; Section~3.1 also shows this is where the omnibus loses the most power relative to its best component.

\subsubsection*{S1.3 Moderate Dense Signal (MDS)}

In an MDS pathway, many genes show modest but consistent association, with no single dominant gene. The ranked profile shows a broad shoulder of moderately small $p$-values rather than a sharp peak, and pathway-level evidence reflects the combined effects of many genes. Methods that aggregate consistent moderate signals (Fisher’s and Stouffer’s) should be most sensitive; soft TFisher should also perform well when enrichment is concentrated in a moderately sized subset.

Biologically, MDS is expected in pathways whose output depends on many interacting components rather than a single effector, such as cytokine-driven immune signalling. In TNF$\alpha$/IL-1$\beta$ to NF-$\kappa$B signalling, activity depends on receptor engagement, adaptor recruitment, kinase cascades, feedback, and downstream transcriptional and post-transcriptional control \citep{Jiang2021CytoSig,Megha2021Inflammation,Verstrepen2008NFkB,vanLoo2011NFkB,Cargnello2011MAPK}. Variation in such pathways is therefore more likely to produce many moderate perturbations than one large-effect change.

\subsubsection*{S1.4 Weak Diffuse Enrichment (WDE)}

In a WDE pathway, most genes are individually non-significant, but the pathway shows a modest shift toward stronger association. The ranked profile displays a gentle, pathway-wide deflection rather than a peak, indicating a weak but widespread signal. Methods that capture distributed enrichment across many genes, especially Stouffer’s and Fisher’s, should be most sensitive \citep{deLeeuw2015}; extreme-tail statistics stay near the null because no gene is extreme.

Biologically, WDE reflects a highly polygenic architecture in which pathway signal comes from many small effects. Human height is a classic example, shaped by thousands of variants with small individual effects \citep{Guo2018HeightGWAS,Yengo2022HeightSaturation}. In such settings, pathways such as TGF-$\beta$ signalling, Hedgehog signalling, and growth-plate extracellular-matrix processes may be detectable mainly through cumulative pathway enrichment rather than strong single-gene signals \citep{LangoAllen2010Height,Guo2018HeightGWAS}.

\subsubsection*{S1.5 Mixed Signal Architecture (MSA)}

In an MSA pathway, one or a few genes show strong association while many others provide moderate support. The ranked profile has a sharp leading peak and a moderate shoulder, placing MSA between sparse SSS and coordinated MDS. Methods that capture both features should be most sensitive, especially adaptive soft TFisher and Fisher’s method; ACAT mainly tracks the peak. In the family-wise simulation of Section~3.1, Fisher was the most powerful for MSA at all pathway sizes, with soft TFisher close behind.

Biologically, MSA arises when a few core regulatory or structural genes with large effects sit within a broader network of auxiliary components. Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol metabolism illustrates this: rare, high-effect variants in \textit{LDLR}, \textit{APOB} and \textit{PCSK9} cause monogenic familial hypercholesterolaemia, while population-level LDL-C variation also reflects many common alleles with small to moderate effects in other lipid genes \citep{Umlai2025LDLC,Peloso2022WGSbloodlipids}. GWAS further implicate genes involved in lipoprotein metabolism and remodelling, including \textit{ABCA1}, \textit{LIPC}, \textit{CETP}, \textit{PLTP}, \textit{ANGPTL3} and \textit{SORT1} \citep{Chasman2009LipoproteinProfiles}. Several are among the established genes used to evaluate the candidate-gene score in Section~3.5.

\subsubsection*{S1.6 How the archetypes were simulated}

The simulation in Section~3.1 (Section~2.6, item~6) uses a shared-universe design: $3{,}000$ genes in independent blocks of $50$ with exchangeable within-block correlation $\rho=0.3$. From the first $600$ genes, we randomly draw a family of $K=150$ overlapping pathways of size $m\in\{5,20,50\}$. Exactly one pathway carries signal; the other $149$ are null.

Signal is a layered mean shift on the gene-level $z$ scale (Table~\ref{tab:archetype_summary}, last column): SSS shifts $15\%$ of genes by $3.0$; SGP $5\%$ by $4.0$; MDS $60\%$ by $1.1$; WDE $90\%$ by $0.55$; and MSA shifts $10\%$ by $1.8$ plus $60\%$ by $1.1$. Each layer contains at least one gene, so at $m=5$ the sparse archetypes shift exactly one gene, and MSA shifts one gene strongly and three moderately. Shifted genes are resampled each replicate, so signal does not consistently hit the same genes or LD blocks. All shifts are multiplied by an effect scale in $\{0.5,1.0,1.5,2.0,2.5,3.0\}$, spanning undetectable to saturated regimes at the considered pathway sizes.

The null bank, five components, soft TFisher grid, undefined-component rule, and omnibus follow Section~2. For each archetype, $m$, and effect scale, we record (for each component and the omnibus) the fraction of replicates in which the signal pathway exceeds its own Westfall--Young FWER threshold at $\alpha=0.05$. A cell is informative if the best component’s power is in $(0.05,0.98)$; Table~\ref{tab:archverify} winners and the first/second/lower-place counts in Section~3.1 are computed over informative cells. Cell regret is the best single-component power minus the omnibus power.
